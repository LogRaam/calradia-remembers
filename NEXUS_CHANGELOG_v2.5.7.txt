Version 2.5.7

Your saves from 2.5.6 carry over untouched. Every fix here came from a player's own log or a player's own report. Thank you for sending them, and in one case for doing most of the diagnosis as well.

Fixed
- The chat window appears when it should. This is the important one. It was reported like this: "the chat overlay yields on NPC-initiated conversations and stays hidden for the rest of the session. The mod itself works end to end, profiles load, vanilla lines are seeded, the AI returns a full response, but the overlay is never drawn. Once it happens, it stays broken for every subsequent conversation until the game is restarted." The chat is drawn over whatever is already on screen, and it has to step aside politely when a real menu opens over it, or that menu would appear and refuse to answer your mouse. It decided that far too crudely: it simply counted how many things were on screen when it opened, and treated any growth as a menu. The ordinary dialogue window is one of those things, and when an NPC walks up to YOU it arrives a moment after the chat rather than before it. So the chat stepped aside for the very window it exists to replace, and it never came back, because that window stays for the whole conversation. A chat you cannot see is a chat you cannot close, which is why every later conversation was refused too. The same mistake also hid the chat whenever you opened it inside a tavern or a settlement menu. It now asks what is actually there rather than how much of it there is, and anything already on screen when the chat opens is understood to be the scene it was drawn over, not something on top of it.
- A lord's letter offering coin for your prisoner tells you why you can only decline. Reported: "Lords are writing me to offer some good sums of coin for the release of prisoners, and my only option is to decline the letter. I wanted to accept an offer, but was forced to decline." The rule itself was right, since you cannot sell someone who is no longer in your prison, but the mod knew that and said nothing, so a lapsed offer and a broken feature looked identical from where you were sitting. The letter now names the reason: that the captive has been freed, ransomed, escaped, or was lost with a fief. The mod also records, when the letter first arrives, whether the trade was still possible, so we can find out whether these offers usually reach you too late and speed them up if they do.
- A companion no longer declares feelings the game then refuses. Reported: "an NPC will confess their feelings to me even when my intimacy level with them isn't high enough. It creates a disconnect: the NPC is expressing affection, yet the system displays a message stating that intimacy is insufficient." The bond a companion needs before confiding was moved onto your Relationship Pacing setting in 2.5.6, but two other places in the same story were still measuring against the old fixed number, so on Warm or Storyteller a companion could declare themselves and be turned down in the same breath. One bond, one number, for the whole arc, at every pacing.

Changed
- Characters no longer promise you things the game will refuse. This is the companion problem above, seen from further back and answered for everything rather than for one arc. When something is not possible right now, the mod used to simply not mention it, and silence does not read to an AI as "forbidden", it reads as "nobody said anything", so the character invented the permission and you met the contradiction a moment later. Now, when a lord could lend you his voice at court or his soldiers and has decided he will not trust you with either yet, he is told so before he answers, and he tells you himself, in his own words, instead of agreeing to something that then does not happen.
- Standing no longer manufactures affection. A clan of good standing, or a name made in the arena, used to tell every character who could be interested in you to raise the subject of a match without waiting for you to bring it up, whatever they actually felt about you. Your standing still makes you a credible prospect, but the feeling now has to be real. In the same spirit, a character who likes you but does not yet love you will flirt, tease and answer an advance honestly, and will not talk about love, belonging, or a life together, because it is not true yet. How deep a bond that takes follows your Relationship Pacing setting, so a Storyteller campaign reaches these scenes considerably sooner than a Grounded one.

A note on that last change: it touches every conversation with a character who could be drawn to you, and it is new. If a character now feels colder than they should, or if a courtship you were expecting no longer comes up, turn on logging in the mod options, reproduce it once, and send the log. The mod writes a great deal about what it is doing, and every fix in this release was found that way.
