Version 2.5.9

Your saves from 2.5.8 carry over untouched. This is a big release and it has one idea behind most of it: a character should know what a person in their position would obviously know, and should not be handed a fact they have no way of possessing. Until now the mod told every character roughly the same things about the world and left the rest to the AI, which does not read silence as "you do not know this" - it reads silence as "nobody mentioned it" and fills the gap. So a governor could be unaware he governs, and a villager could discuss the war like a courier who had just ridden in from it.

What a character knows

- What reaches a character is now composed from their actual life. Their house and its standing, their own people, the seat they hold and how they hold it, what their house can afford, the hour and the sky and the ground they are standing on, the state of their own body, the market they trade in, the underworld they touch, news of the realm, the post they hold in your party, the ventures and contracts they have entered into. Each of these is a separate piece that is present only when that character genuinely has it, so a farmhand and a governor no longer receive the same shape of world.

- Knowing something is a matter of DEGREE, not a yes or no. Word of a war reaches a lord in his hall sooner and in more detail than it reaches a villager at the end of a valley, and both of them hear it before a prisoner in a cell does. The mod now says how far and how clearly a thing has reached this particular person, rather than either handing them the fact or hiding it. The frontier of a character's ignorance is a slope, not a wall.

- A governor is told he governs. Reported by fkasad. Nothing in the prompt had ever named the post, so a man appointed to run a town might talk about it as somebody else's business. He now knows the seat he holds, and the same goes for the office you gave a companion in your own party: the mod reads who is actually doing the job, not who was once appointed.

- Characters may still invent, and now there is a switch for it. An NPC filling a gap with a plausible invention is part of what makes them feel alive, and it is also how a rumour starts. When it is on, an invention can become a rumour a third party can later repeat back to you. It ships OFF, so nothing changes unless you go looking for it in the mod options.

- Compact Prompt mode got smaller again. 2.5.8 cut the action list from the prompt; this release cuts it down to what the character was actually taught THIS turn instead of the full catalogue, which is a further saving of roughly 3,000 characters. There is also a fix worth naming because it was mine: the in-world clock was being written into a part of the prompt that is cached between turns, so every passing hour was throwing away 27% of the cache and paying to send the whole prompt again.

Companions, briefings and group conversations

- The private word before a meeting actually works now. This shipped in 2.5.6 barely played and it did not hold up: closing the briefing dropped you back on the map with the meeting gone, or into a loading screen, or crashed. The whole thing has been rebuilt around one idea - what was said in private becomes a PRIVATE MEMORY on the people who were in the room, and a private memory is never readable by anyone else. Closing the briefing now hands you straight into the meeting it was for, with the people you briefed already at your side, and the person who waited speaks first. They know you stepped aside with your own people; they do not know a word of what was said, and they may make of that what they like.

- A briefing plans; the meeting acts. Nothing mechanical happens inside a private word any more. A companion briefed to charm a lord was answering with what read as a vow, and the game recorded him swearing to fight at your side in battle for 21 days - a war oath born in a conversation about a seduction. Whatever is decided in a briefing now happens out in the meeting, where it can be seen.

- Bringing a third person into a conversation works. yozakura12, in the same report: "during a standard three-person conversation, if you try to bring the third person into the discussion, they fail to join in." They were not being ignored by the AI; they were not in the scene at all. The list of who is actually PRESENT was built at the moment the conversation opened, and a companion you bring along is resolved a moment later, so they sat in the room without ever being added to it. Every attempt to give them the floor then quietly failed. The code that adds a late arrival had been written a fortnight earlier and never once called.

- A conversation does not end on a question nobody answers. When several characters are in a scene, one of them could put a direct question to another and the floor would come straight back to you with it hanging - you had to spend a turn saying something like "I wait and watch" to make the beat finish. It finishes on its own now, once, and then returns to you.

- And a character no longer answers for somebody else. In a group scene the AI could write the whole scene rather than its own part, complete with lines for YOU that you never said. In one case those invented lines said out loud, to the very lord it was meant to stay hidden from, what had been agreed in private. A reply is one character's turn, and anything it writes for anyone else is now cut before you see it.

- Speaking to a single companion no longer says "We are listening."

- A lord can take one of your people into his service. This is the mirror of every other recruiting verb in the mod: they all bring somebody in, and this is the only one that gives somebody away. If you offer a companion to a lord and you both agree, they leave your clan for his household. Your spouse cannot be given, nor your own blood, nor a companion currently governing one of your fiefs, and if you have several companions present the lord must name which one.

Fixed

- A partner whose own people accept the arrangement stopped being ground down by it. yozakura12: "Even if an NPC from the Empire accepts polyamory, their affinity continues to drop. Since there is no specific information regarding jealousy, it is possible that a recent update - intended to align views on romance with the culture - has caused an internal conflict or inconsistency." He was right that something contradicted itself, and the word to notice in his report is CONTINUES. The culture rule was working: where an exclusive partner takes 10, 25 or 40 for a flirt, an intimacy or a marriage, a partner from a sharing culture was already taking only 3, 6 or 8, because by their own custom the grievance is RANK AND ATTENTION, not betrayal. What was missing is that nothing ever stopped it. Three at a time, across a long campaign, grinds somebody who ACCEPTS the arrangement all the way down to hatred. Being set below another consort should cost you warmth; it must not manufacture an enemy out of a practice their own people keep. There is a floor now, at neutral: they may cool completely and go no further, and once they are there the slight costs no more regard at all - though they will still remember it and may still write to you about it, because being overlooked is worth saying whatever the number does. A real betrayal is a different matter and keeps its old reach.

- And such a partner can now tell you WHY the number is falling. The custom that reconciles the two - by our practice this is no betrayal, but my standing among them is mine to mind - existed in the code and fed only the part that picks who is most aggrieved. It never reached the character, so they could not name what was happening to them and you had a falling bar with no explanation anywhere.

- Conversations reach local and self-hosted models that were refusing them. The mod was sending a message shape that only some chat templates forgive. If you run your own server and a conversation simply never came back, try again.

- The action interpreter no longer sends your server a model name it cannot know. Every other role had been fixed for this; this one was missed.

Notes

- This is a LARGE release and two of its parts are new enough that they have not been through many hands. The knowledge composition touches every conversation in the game, and the lord-takes-a-companion offer has never once been accepted in a live test - not because it failed, but because the lords we asked said no, which is their right. If something reads oddly, a log attached to a report here is worth more than a description; the mod writes one to Documents\Mount and Blade II Bannerlord\Logs\CalradiaRemembers.log when logging is on in the mod options.

- Nothing in this release changes your save format, and nothing needs a new game.
