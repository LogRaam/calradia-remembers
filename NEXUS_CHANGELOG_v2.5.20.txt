Version 2.5.20

Four changes you can feel, one of them a conversation that has quietly been impossible since July, plus groundwork that does not change the game but should make the next months of fixes faster and safer.

New

- The conversation you stepped out of is still there when you come back. Roleplaying takes longer than a battle, and a negotiation often outlasts a sitting: you closed the chat, saved, quit, and on reload the character remembered talking to you but not a word of what was said, so the bargain started from the beginning. Reopen the chat with the same person on the same day and the exchange comes back, both on screen and in what the character knows, so you carry on from the last thing said. Open a chat with somebody else first and it is gone, because that conversation is over. Only the last one is kept, and only for that day.

- You can duel in a castle. Lords are met in castles more often than anywhere else, and a courtyard is as good a place to cross blades as a town square. The duel was offered there and then quietly refused: you agreed to fight and nothing happened. It works now.

Fixed

- You can ask a lord what it would take to make things right, and he will tell you. A character holding a grievance against you was always meant to be able to name the price of forgiveness: a gift, a deed, an apology, or simply time. That answer is also the only way the game records what would mend the grudge. It turned out the action was never registered where the game teaches it, so no character could ever give it. It has been unreachable since it shipped in July, and it works now.

- Lords judge you on the relationship you actually built with them. Recruiting a lord into your clan was weighed on the base game's relation number, while the equivalent checks for notables and for prisoners used the standing you build by talking to them. A lord you had befriended over a whole campaign in conversation could refuse you to his face because the base game had never heard of any of it. All three now agree.

- A rescued lord's infant children no longer feel a debt to you. Freeing someone made their whole family grateful, babies included, who then remembered a rescue they could not have understood.

- A payment cap that existed in five places now exists in one. The limit on what a single transfer can carry was written out separately in several files, and a blackmail demand above it could take your gold without buying anything.

For modders

- A total conversion can now set its own calendar. setting.json accepts daysPerSeason and seasonsPerYear beside the world name and adjective it already supported. Until now a conversion could rename the world, but its year was still 84 days long, so every "a season ago" a character said was wrong for it. Leave the fields out and nothing changes.

Notes

- Your existing saves load exactly as they are and nothing needs a new game. Saving now also keeps the words of your last conversation, which an older version of the mod would simply ignore.
