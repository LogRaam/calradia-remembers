Version 2.5.14

One addition for anyone running a local model, asked for on this page by fkasad, and three fixes: one reported by a modder who was careful enough to ask before working around it, one found while answering a player's question, and one reported by a player who got paid for a job the game then refused to close.

New

- The mod's background work can now be sent to a different model from your conversations. In the mod options you will find a Background model field, and, for an OpenAI-Compatible server, a Background endpoint and API key to go with it. Leave them blank and absolutely nothing changes: your summaries and memory compression go exactly where they always did.

  What this is for. The mod makes two very different kinds of call. One is the conversation, which is a long prompt that stays mostly the same from turn to turn. The other is its background work: summarising a conversation after you close it, compressing an NPC's memory when it gets long, reading a transcript for claims about the world. Those are short, mechanical, and look nothing like a conversation.

  On a hosted service that costs you a little money and nothing else. On a local server it can cost you much more than that, and fkasad worked out why before anyone here did. A local server keeps the work it has already done for a prompt and reuses it when the next prompt starts the same way. Two kinds of prompt with nothing in common, arriving one after the other, throw away each other's work every single time. Nothing the mod sends can prevent that, because the bookkeeping belongs to the server. Sending them to a different model is what prevents it.

  So: point the background work at something small and cheap and leave your good model to do the talking. A 4B model is plenty to summarise a conversation.

  One thing is deliberately NOT background, in case you wonder why it still uses your main model: the illuminated chronicle. It runs after the fact like the rest, but you read it, so it stays where the prose is.

Fixed

- A lord can no longer be offered a member of your family as though they were a retainer. Reported by reminensce, who is building a bridge submod: "it's possible for extended family members or married-in nobles to be given to and/or taken by other lords. Is this the intended behavior or an actual semantic error?"

  It was an error, and he had read it exactly right. The 2.5.9 notes promised that your spouse and your own blood could never be given away, and "your own blood" had been taken to mean your parents, your children and your siblings, and nobody else. A house that has run for a generation holds grandchildren, nephews, cousins, and the husbands and wives of all of them. A daughter-in-law is in your clan, is not your blood and is not your spouse, so she passed every check there was.

  Family now means what you would mean by it: anyone who shares a forebear with you within three generations, and anyone married to one of them, read from the game's own family tree. It decides both whether the offer is made at all and whether it goes through, so the two can never disagree. Companions, and anyone who joined your house rather than being born or married into it, can still be offered exactly as before, including a turned nemesis or a prisoner who swore to you.

- You can no longer be taught a skill by someone who knows less of it than you do. Found while answering Zinced121212, who asked on this page whether characters are aware of skills at all. Anyone with real mastery of a skill can teach it to you if you ask, but nothing ever checked how good YOU already were: a player at 300 in Medicine could be taught Medicine by a companion at 100, and be paid in experience for it. A teacher now has to know more of it than you do, and a character only offers lessons in what they could actually show you. If you ask anyway, you are told plainly that there is nothing to learn from them there.

- Handing a lord the prisoner he asked for now always completes his request. Reported by Lordfadooboo: a Khuzait lord asked him to deliver a Nord lord in chains, he brought one, typed out the handover, "the character spoke as if i completed the quest, i even got paid, but the quest still remained on my board, unable to be completed."

  There are two ways a prisoner changes hands in a conversation, delivering one that was asked for and selling one, and only the delivery ever closed the request. Which one happened depended on how the character took your words. Worse, the only prisoner option in the offer bag was worded as a sale, which pushed straight towards the one that never closes anything. Now, a prisoner handed to someone who asked for them completes the request however it was phrased, and you are paid the promised reward once, not a sale price as well. When the prisoner you pick in the bag is one this person asked for, the bag offers the handover instead of a sale. And a prisoner held in your own dungeon now counts for a delivery, as they already did for a sale.

  If you have a request stuck this way from before, the console command cr.quest_void with its number (see cr.quests) clears it without any penalty.

Notes

- Nothing here changes your save format and nothing needs a new game.

- Still open from the same discussion: a cleared looter camp counts for as much as a city falling, so lords congratulate you on housekeeping, and a grievance has no notion of ever having been answered. Both are understood and neither is a one-line fix.
