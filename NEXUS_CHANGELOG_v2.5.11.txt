Version 2.5.11

Your saves from 2.5.10 carry over untouched. Six fixes, five of them reported here by name in the two days since 2.5.10 went up. Thank you for the logs, and in one case for the two prompts side by side. Nothing in this release would have been found without them.

Fixed

- The game no longer crashes when you challenge someone to a duel. Reported by yozakura12, twice: once on 2.5.9 and again on 2.5.10, the version that was meant to have fixed it. The second report is the one that found it, because his log contained the line that mattered: "returned to campaign map". He was AT a town, standing on it, not inside it. The mod was asking three different questions about where the duel should happen, none of which was the one that decides anything, and all three answer yes for a party merely parked on a settlement. So the game was asked to open a town interior from outside the walls, which it cannot do, and it died loading the scene. There is one question now, and it asks whether you are actually inside. From outside the walls the duel is fought in the open field instead, which is where a man standing at the gate would fight anyway. If you want the duel in the streets, walk into the town first.

- A companion who asks for a word now gets one. Reported by Lordfadooboo and, a week later in almost the same words, by Zinced121212: "i enter a city and it says (companion) wants to talk to you, i say yes, and nothing happens." Both were in a town, and that is the whole of it. When a conversation is staged out on the map the game registers it immediately; inside a settlement an entire interior scene has to load first. The mod was timing the second with a stopwatch written for the first, so it declared a perfectly healthy scene dead in a sixth of a second, opened the window over the town menu, and the arriving scene carried it off. Both cases keep a limit, because a staging that never resolves would block every later meeting, but they no longer share a clock.

- The mod now says so when something you asked for does not happen. This is the reason the one above took a month. Every branch that could swallow an answered popup wrote its account to the log file, which is switched off by default, so the players who reported it had nothing to send and we had nothing to read. A dead end you actually experienced now puts one line on screen whatever your settings say. If you ever answer a prompt and see nothing follow, that line is what to report.

- Someone you wrote back to remembers that you wrote back. Reported by Zivkyn: "Love interest sends me four letters, i write back to each one, she responds to each one. I return to talk to her and shes mad i didnt write back." Her memory agreed with him. A letter arriving from her was recorded, and a letter you sent her unprompted was recorded, but a REPLY to one of her letters was recorded nowhere at all. She held four memories of writing to you and none of being answered, and that is what she spoke from. Replies are now remembered like everything else, and what you actually said in them is summarised into the memory rather than just the fact that you answered.

- A character no longer asks how a task is coming along thirty seconds after agreeing it. Reported by raphareish: "after accepting a quest and talking about the deal that was just made, NPCs behave like it happened hours before on the same day, even though the chat has never been closed." The task was being listed to them as an outstanding errand, complete with a note that they may ask how it fares, the moment it was agreed. A task settled at the table now reads as exactly that, and they know you have not had a chance to begin it. They still know what was agreed; what has gone is the impression that you took a week over it.

- A lord no longer writes to you as though you rode in his retinue. Reported by zhenja1zhenja1, about a letter that arrived after he beat that lord in a tournament. Nothing was inverted here: the mod knew perfectly well that he owed that lord nothing. The trouble is what it did with that answer, which was to say nothing at all, and an AI does not read silence as "there is no relationship", it reads it as "nobody mentioned it" and fills the gap. A character is now told plainly where you stand with them, including when the answer is that nothing binds you to each other, which is the commonest case in the game. As a side effect this costs slightly less per turn than it did, because the standing is now sent once per conversation instead of on every reply.

Notes

- The duel fix and the companion-audience fix are both reasoned from what the logs say rather than reproduced here, so if either still misbehaves for you, a log attached to a report is worth more than a description. The mod writes one to Documents\Mount and Blade II Bannerlord\Logs\CalradiaRemembers.log when logging is on in the mod options.

- Nothing here changes your save format and nothing needs a new game.
