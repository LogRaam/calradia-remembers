Version 2.5.3

Your saves from 2.5.2 carry over untouched.

New
- Battles are remembered by the people who were in them. A fight no longer leaves one vague line in an NPC's memory. The characters who were there remember what you actually did: who you bested, who put you down, and how heavy the fighting was, but only when it was heavy enough to be worth mentioning. And they each speak of it from where they stood. The lord you beat says you bested him; the man who put you down says he did it himself; everyone else speaks as a witness. Nothing is invented, and nobody is called dead unless they truly died, so no one mourns a lord you can go and visit in your own dungeon. Face the same enemy sword to sword often enough and a standing rivalry forms, warm or sour depending on the kind of man he is, exactly as it does in the tournament lists.
- A great feat travels on its own. Beat someone well above your weight, or a name the whole realm knows, and word gets out: a lord who was nowhere near the battle can bring it up as something he has heard. Ordinary victories stay with the men who saw them. The same goes for a rated win in the arena, which is what finally lets a lord who was not at the tournament congratulate you on it.
- You can turn on the person you are talking to. If a conversation goes somewhere ugly, you can rob them, beat them, or kill them. Killing is off by default and must be switched on in the mod options; robbing and beating are always available. None of it is a formality. How it ends depends on how the two of you compare as fighters and on whether they are alone, so someone with guards at their side is far harder to reach, and you can simply fail. A killing always asks you to confirm before anything happens, because it is the AI that decides a killing was narrated, and that judgement is not safe enough to spend a character on. Say no and nothing whatsoever is recorded. (Requested by a player.)
- And the world answers for it. The law is the game's own crime rating against the victim's realm, the same one that turns guards hostile and puts a bounty on you, in the engine's own time. The law hears something even when nobody was watching, so getting someone alone is an advantage but never a free pass, and a failed attempt still costs you, so attempted murder is not a free roll you can keep retrying. Their kin carry a grudge, the victim themselves never forgets, your standing suffers when the deed was seen, and word spreads: a killing always becomes news, a robbery or a beating only when there was someone there to see it.

Fixed
- Asking a lord to ride with you works again. It had been refused for every lord since v2.4.4. Bandit clans counted as realms at war, and Calradia is always at war with bandits, so the rule meant to keep lords with their faction's armies during a war was turning everyone down, always.
- Renown penalties actually take renown now. Every penalty in the mod had been doing nothing at all. The game's own function for granting renown silently throws away a negative amount, so five different systems (the scandal of a bastard, a lost duel, being humiliated as a captive, a leaked letter, and now violence) had never moved a single point, despite looking like working code.
- Keyboard shortcuts no longer reach the map from under a panel. Typing a reply to a letter and pressing the face-generator key would open the character editor underneath. The letter book and the Balance of Power sit over the live map, which kept reading its own shortcuts straight through them.
- The AI no longer gets backwards who is attacking whom. A player who struck at a character could end up with the exchange reaching for the action meaning the NPC kills the PLAYER. That direction is now pinned down, and the AI is told which actions actually exist for a player's own violence.
- A voice you write for a character no longer fades. If you gave a character a voice in their backstory ("in the voice of Homer"), it held for an exchange or two and then drifted away, because it sat in the opening of the prompt where the closing instructions drowned it out. It is now restated at the end, the same way the narrative style already was.
- The panel zoom is only rewritten when it actually changes, a follow-up to the screen-ratio fix.
