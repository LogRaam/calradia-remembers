[size=4]Why this mod exists[/size]

Calradia Remembers began as a ground-up answer to a simple wish: an LLM-driven roleplay mod whose code I fully own, understand, and can keep alive.

The idea of talking freely to Bannerlord's characters isn't new.. AI Influence showed the community what was possible. But its source is obfuscated: it can't be studied, fixed, or extended by anyone but its author, and when something doesn't work as expected you're stuck waiting. I didn't want to build on a black box. So I wrote Calradia Remembers from scratch, every line my own, with a clean and documented codebase.. a mod that can be maintained, audited, and grown openly for as long as people want to play it.

What came out of that is, I think, more than a clone. The heart of this mod is [b][color=#ffff00]memory[/color][/b]: your NPCs remember.

..no more "0 conversation" with NPC you already had a conversation with.

Three commitments shape every decision in this project:

[b]1. Honour the modding community.[/b] Modding lives on sharing and respect.. between creators and players, and toward the original game. CR is open by design: readable code, extensible architecture, honest release notes about what is tested and what is not. No black boxes. No hollow promises.

[b]2. Enrich Bannerlord, not replace it.[/b] The goal is not a parallel roleplay world disconnected from the game. NPCs in CR remember, react, and speak.. but they remain lords and ladies of Calradia, not characters in a novel. What they promise lands in the quest journal. The bonds they forge shift the political map. CR amplifies what is already there; it does not override it.

[b]3. Code that deserves the ambition.[/b] A mod that lasts needs architecture that holds. Pure logic isolated from the engine, injected seams, tests that document expected behaviour.. not implementation details. When a behaviour changes unintentionally, a test catches it before a player does.

[line]
[size=4]What it does[/size]

Every notable NPC in Calradia keeps a private, persistent memory of you.. stored inside your save, not a side file. You don't pick lines from a menu: you [i][color=#c9daf8]type whatever you want[/color][/i], and the character answers in their own voice, shaped by who they are, what your two factions are doing, and everything that has passed between you before.

Make an oath and break it, and they'll remember the betrayal months later. Earn a lord's trust through deeds, and his warmth is real. Insult a lady in front of her peers, and word gets around. Conversations leave marks.. on relations, on reputation, sometimes on your purse.. and those marks last!

[line]
[size=4]Features[/size]

[b][color=#e69138]Persistent NPC memory[/color][/b]
Each character records notable events between you.. first meetings, agreements, betrayals, intimate moments, partings.. stamped with the in-game day and carried forward in your save.  When you meet again, they speak as someone who lived through all of it. A built-in, LLM-driven memory compressor keeps long histories from bloating while protecting the moments that matter.

[b][color=#e69138]Free-text conversation, in character[/color][/b]
A clean overlay chat.. no dialogue trees. NPCs reply with the weight of their clan, their liege, and their personality. They judge you by deeds, not flattery, and they don't casually give up their secrets.

[b][color=#e69138]Woven into the vanilla game[/color][/b]
The mod plugs straight into Bannerlord's own dialogue: walk up to any lord exactly as you always do, and a "[i]We need to talk…[/i]" option sits among the normal choices. Pick it and the character greets you.. with a real vanilla facial expression that matches how they feel about you, warm, neutral, or cold.. and an opening line that itself varies with their regard and character rather than a single fixed phrase.. then the chat opens to continu the conversation with a LLM. Close it and you're back in the ordinary conversation. Nothing native is removed: trade, quests, and every vanilla option are still right where you left them. The mod gives the characters a voice; it doesn't replace your game.

[b][color=#e69138]Living personalities[/color][/b]
Personalities and traits drawn from each hero, relationship awareness (friends, rivals, family, liege), and a sense of standing.. a noble of a great clan does not speak to you the way a landless adventurer does.

[b][color=#e69138]Ask about other people[/color][/b]
Mention another character and the NPC answers from their own vantage point.. friend, rival, kin, or stranger. Ask where someone is, and if it is a person they would plausibly know.. a clansman, a close friend, someone of their own faction.. they may tell you, as hearsay: "[i]Last I heard, she was riding toward Ocs Hall.[/i]" A distant stranger they cannot place, and no one invents a whereabouts they were never told.

[b][color=#e69138]Culture matters (and it's yours to shape)[/color][/b]
Calradia's peoples don't all think alike, and the characters reflect it. A Battanian, where women fight, inherit, and lead, meets you very differently from a Vlandian or Aserai lord who expects modesty and deference, while the progressive Southern Empire under Empress Rhagaea takes female authority in stride.  A [b][color=#d5a6bd]female player character[/color][/b] runs into authentic cultural skepticism in the patriarchal factions, not blank generic politeness. All of this ships ready-made with the mod.. and, like the rest of the prompt, it's [i]yours to rewrite[/i]: the cultural rules live in `cultural_traditions.json` and `behavior_guidelines.txt` (see [i][color=#c9daf8]Make it your own[/color][/i] below), so you can soften them, sharpen them, or reshape a whole culture or create a new world however you like.

[b][color=#e69138]Real consequences[/color][/b]
What's said in conversation can change the world: relation shifts, gold given or taken, and [b][color=#b6d7a8]informal quests[/color][/b] an NPC may offer you on their own initiative.. verified by [i][color=#93c47d]actual[/color][/i] game events before any reward is paid. No reward for a story you merely tell; only for a deed genuinely done.

[b][color=#e69138]Join a kingdom as a mercenary through conversation[/color][/b]
A lord who wants reliable swords.. or a player who needs a contract.. can negotiate mercenary service right there in the chat window. When your clan carries no existing obligation, any lord of a kingdom can offer you a contract on behalf of their realm, and you can propose it first if the mood calls for it. When you both clearly agree, your clan enrolls immediately: no separate menu, no lord's hall required.

[b][color=#e69138]Recruit companions through conversation[/color][/b]
Meet a wanderer in a tavern and convince them to ride with you.. in your own words, not through a menu. The asking price is the same one the vanilla game would charge.. and it is negotiable: impress them and they may come down, lowball them and they will hold firm or walk away. Fine titles do not waive the fee, and dressing the hire up as "joining my clan" will not get you out of paying.. a deal is a deal on the steppe. When you do strike one, they truly join your party, coin changes hands once, and the bargain itself becomes part of your shared history.
[i]New.. pay in goods, not just coin.[/i] Short on denars? Ask whether something else might seal the deal, and a wanderer may take payment in kind. A bag icon in the chat opens a picker of your party inventory.. whole stacks, each shown with its value.. so you can settle the hire with wares instead of money. Goods are reckoned at roughly double the coin price, and a proud or hard-headed character may refuse the barter and hold out for denars, in character. You can also simply [b]give an NPC an item[/b] from your inventory as a gift in any conversation, and watch them react to it. And beside the bag sits a [b]Give Gold[/b] button.. name a sum and hand over coin on the spot, whether as a gift or to settle a lord who asked you to bring them a purse of denars.

[b][color=#e69138]Quests that actually resolve[/color][/b]
The tasks NPCs hand you aren't throwaway flavor text.. each one is mirrored into Bannerlord's real quest journal, and it always closes cleanly. Complete it (confirmed by [color=#ffe599][i]actual[/i][/color] game events, never by the AI simply saying so), let its deadline lapse, abandon it honestly, or watch its target stop making sense.. every outcome resolves the journal entry, and a self-check on every load force-closes anything left dangling. [color=#f1c232]No ghost quests piling up in your log, and no errand that can never be finished[/color].. a common rough edge when an LLM is bolted onto the vanilla quest system, designed out from the start here.

[b][color=#e69138]Bargains beyond coin[/color][/b]
A lord's price is not always denars. One may agree to ride with you only if you [b]hand over an enemy captive[/b].. a rival lord they want in chains: give over one you already hold, or take it as a quest to go capture them and bring them back. Another, with cause against a faction, may make their service conditional on [b]you declaring war[/b] on it.. "rid me of the Vlandians and I am yours". Both are honoured only by the [i][color=#93c47d]real deed[/color][/i].. a true prisoner handed over, a war your own faction actually declares.. never by a promise. The game prices and verifies every such bargain; the talk is yours, the arithmetic is the game's. And the bargain runs both ways.. speak with a [b]captive lord[/b] and they may plead for their freedom: set free one [i]you[/i] hold in exchange for goodwill, gold, or service, or, if another lord holds them, agree to [b]break them out[/b].. a rescue paid only when you truly free them in the field.

[b][color=#e69138]Your house, remembered across generations[/color][/b]
When your character dies and an heir takes up the banner, Calradia does not forget. The lords you dealt with [b]inherit[/b] their standing toward your house.. the debts, the alliances, the grudges.. and speak to your heir of what came before: "[i]your father helped me at the siege of Epicrotea.. I owe your house that debt[/i]," or "[i]your mother's broken word still stings[/i]." The private history of the dead stays buried with them; your heir inherits a reputation, not a diary, and the bond with each lord begins anew from there. Your dynasty carries its name forward.

[b][color=#e69138]Multi-character scenes[/color][/b]
Other lords present in the same hall can overhear, react in their own voice, and take sides.. and who is present actually makes sense: the smith from the market square will not be attending your audience in the keep. Bring up to two of your companions to a meeting (those walking beside you in town join naturally), or go alone. Everything is handled in the conversation itself: address a specific witness directly, ask one of your companions to leave you alone with the NPC in your own words, or request a private audience.. and the NPC decides whether to clear the room. And those who stood in on the talk [b]remember it[/b]: speak with one of them later, alone, and they recall that they were there and what passed between you.

[b][color=#e69138]Talk to the locals[/color][/b]
Walk up to any townsperson, villager, or castle servant.. anyone without a title.. and ask what's going on. A short LLM conversation opens with a commoner whose name and trade come from the local culture. What they know is what the street knows: who rules the settlement, how times have been, which lords are passing through, recent deaths and captures, and the mood of the market. No memory is kept between conversations.. every encounter is a fresh face. The only way coin changes hands is if they think a piece of news is worth paying for.

[b][color=#e69138]A world with news.. and NPCs who've heard it[/color][/b]
Battles, sieges, towns changing hands, noble marriages, executions, declarations of war.. all of it is quietly recorded as it happens, and reaches people the way news really travels. Talk to a lord and they may bring up recent events they would plausibly know of.. what their own realm is caught up in, what happened near them, what is still fresh.. spoken as [i]hearsay[/i], never as omniscience. A Sturgian lord will not recite yesterday's distant Aserai gossip; what reaches someone depends on distance, faction, and how recent it is. And the further word has had to travel, the [i]less sure[/i] it arrives.. a faraway rumour comes wrapped in real doubt ("[i]they say, though none here can vouch for it..[/i]"), and the teller may have a detail wrong, while news from a lord's own region or realm is told plainly. The townsfolk's street rumours are drawn from real events too. And the lords who fought beside you.. or against you.. simply remember how you acquitted yourself, instead of waiting for you to boast of it. The world also speaks of [b][color=#ffe599]you[/color][/b]: a lord who was nowhere near, but to whom word has carried your deeds, may bring them up to your face.. attributed to you, hedged as hearsay.. and they greet you accordingly. Storm a town or put a rival's kin to death and lords of the wounded realm meet you with wariness or fear; do their side a service and it earns you respect. You feel your reputation arrive in the room before you speak.

Word of [b]disgrace[/b] travels the same way: affront a lord steeply in front of witnesses and the shame itself becomes a rumour others may have heard, rather than evaporating the moment you part. And when two lords of the same crown nurse real bad blood, word of their [b]rivalry[/b] circulates as ambient gossip.. so a lord may volunteer which of their own peers are at each other's throats.

[b][color=#e69138]Court intrigue.. plots between lords[/color][/b]
The nobility of Calradia does not only feud in the open. A lord may quietly set a [b]scheme[/b] in motion against a rival they cannot abide.. to [b][color=#d5a6bd]blacken their name[/color][/b] until their peers turn cold, or to [b][color=#d5a6bd]undermine their standing[/color][/b] and bleed away their influence. It unfolds on its own, in secret.. the plot gains ground over the weeks and either ripens into real harm to its target, or is caught and collapses before it can land.

You can take a hand in it. A plotter who trusts you may sound you out in conversation and invite you to help.. and if you throw in with them, their scheme leaps forward. But complicity carries a price: should the affair ever be exposed, or succeed with your hand in it, the wronged party comes to know.. and turns on you. The betrayal cuts deeper when you moved against your own realm or a friend than against a stranger or a standing enemy.

Or you can [b]betray the plotter's confidence[/b]. Seek out the lord the plot is [i]aimed at[/i] and warn them.. they hold no certain knowledge of it themselves, and act only on a credible word from you. Forewarned, they drag the conspiracy into the open and stand a real chance of undoing it. A grateful target becomes a genuine [color=#93c47d]ally[/color]; the exposed plotter, suspecting your hand, a solid [color=#cc0000]enemy[/color].. and how warmly the one regards you and how bitterly the other resents you turns on the kind of people they are.. an honourable lord feels a true debt, a cold and calculating one suspects your motive; a vindictive plotter hates you the harder, a merciful one bears it with more grace.

And every plot that comes to light ripples outward as [i]rumour[/i].. the realm's intrigues become something its people hear of, and gossip about, like everything else.

[b][color=#e69138]Your Chronicle[/color][/b]
Your own deeds are written into a chronicle in the voice of a medieval chronicler.. "[i]In the autumn of the sixth year, the lord {you} met {foe} in open battle near {place}, and by sword and valour put the foe to flight.[/i]" Open it any time from the [b][color=#d5a6bd]Read the Chronicle[/color][/b] button on your own character's Encyclopedia page: a parchment-styled window of your saga, dated and in order. Your legend, written as you live it.

And if you want more than plain entries, an [b][color=#d5a6bd]Illuminate[/color][/b] button in that window has the AI rewrite your whole saga into flowing, period-voiced chronicler's prose.. and it grows with you, weaving each new deed in as it happens rather than starting over. The voice itself is yours to shape through an editable style file (`chronicle_style.txt`), so your chronicle can read as solemn, florid, or terse as you like.

[color=#e69138][b]Letters & visible couriers[/b][/color]
Correspondence has a home of its own now: a dedicated [b][color=#d5a6bd]parchment letter window[/color][/b], the correspondent's portrait at its head and the words set in a clean medieval hand. Read a letter the moment it arrives, write a fresh one, or pen a reply.. then hire a courier who physically crosses the campaign map to carry it.. and who can be intercepted by bandits along the way. NPCs write back, and their replies arrive by courier too, to be read on the same parchment. A reply is sealed and sent at once.. you compose it in your own words, and the answer finds its way back to you in time.

[color=#e69138][b]Encyclopedia integration[/b][/color]
What you learn about a character surfaces on their own encyclopedia page. Alongside their standing toward you, a "[b][color=#d5a6bd]Discoveries[/color][/b]" panel fills in over time: as conversations turn personal, NPCs let slip who they really are.. their temperament, their romantic nature, their preferences, and (with adult content enabled) the more intimate traits and [color=#ff00ff][b]kinks[/b][/color] they reveal. Nothing is handed to you on a plate.. you earn this portrait through trust and talk, one character at a time, and each thing learned is written into their Encyclopedia page for good.

[color=#e69138][b]Story Log (optional)[/b][/color]
Turn it on (in options) and every conversation and letter is written out as a clean, novel-style transcript.. no timestamps, no technical noise.. so you can re-read your campaign like a book.

[b][color=#e69138]Romance & courtship that emerge on their own[/color][/b]
This isn't only an adult feature. From the [color=#8e7cc3][b]Mature[/b][/color] setting up, attraction grows with trust, and an unmarried lord or lady may raise courtship.. or propose marriage outright.. on their [color=#d9d2e9][i]own[/i][/color] initiative when your standing and your shared history warrant it: lead a clan of real rank, or make a name in the tournament lists, and the right people start to notice. You can propose just as freely. How far things go is governed by the Adult Content Level below; that they happen at all is simply Calradia's characters reacting to who you've become.

[b][color=#e69138]Marriage.. family politics, love matches, and letters of proposal[/color][/b]
A match joins two houses, and the house gets a say. Ask the head of a family for the hand of their kin and they weigh your worth against their name.. granting their blessing freely, refusing it, or setting a single worthy deed as its price. Wed into a house [i]without[/i] that blessing and its members take the slight personally and cool toward you; earn it, and the family embraces the union.. though a dissenting cousin may still quietly resent it. Love and ambition, pulling against each other.

When the bond between you and someone runs deep enough.. and you have both earned it.. marriage can also be proposed [i]in conversation[/i] itself: by you, by them, or by both arriving at it together. When you agree, it is real: the game's full marriage takes effect.

Lords also write first. A clan head who regards you well may send a courier proposing a match with one of their eligible kin before you have ever thought to ask. The door can open from their side.

[b][color=#e69138]Consort bonds[/color][/b]
Not every deep commitment needs to be.. or can be.. a legal marriage. When a relationship has grown genuinely intimate, both parties can name it openly: a consort bond is a private, mutual pledge that both of you honour, even though Calradian law does not recognise it. It carries no clan merger, no inheritance claim.. but it is real, and your consort will write to you as a spouse would when you have been apart too long. This path is open even if you are already wed.

[b][color=#e69138]Jealousy.. romance with rivals[/color][/b]
Love is no longer without risk. Court, kiss, or wed someone and a third party with a real stake in them may turn against you.. never a random grudge, always someone with a believable reason: the wronged spouse of the one you courted, your own slighted partner, a close associate who had hoped to marry them first, or an admirer of yours you passed over. They find out the way people really do.. if the moment had [b]witnesses[/b], word travels fast, so flirting with a married lady in front of her kin is a risk you can feel coming. And a [i]private[/i] affair is no longer safe either.. word of it seeps out through the same rumour network and may reach them later all the same, so discretion and distance only buy you time. Days later the consequence lands: their regard for you cools, they remember the slight, and they may send a [b][color=#d5a6bd]letter[/color][/b].. a cold warning, a wounded reproach, or an open threat.. "[i]leave her be.. I mean to marry her one day[/i]."

And it reads the [b][color=#d9d2e9]culture[/color][/b] of the wronged party. Among strictly monogamous peoples a love given elsewhere is a betrayal; among cultures that accept several spouses.. the steppe Khuzait, the Aserai, the highborn of Battania.. no one is outraged that you took another, but now your partners compete for standing and attention, and a great love becomes a harder one to keep. Which peoples hold which view lives in an editable `jealousy_cultures.json`, so a total-conversion or faction mod can set its own customs without touching the code.

[b][color=#e69138]A child kept secret.. and the years it takes to come back[/color][/b]
Romance can leave more than memories. A liaison may quietly produce a [b][color=#d5a6bd]hidden child[/color][/b].. born out of wedlock, the realm only guessing at its father.. and that secret does not stay buried forever. Season by season there is a growing chance it surfaces.. the older the child, the likelier the day.. and when it does, a [b]scandal[/b] breaks: a blow to your renown and your clan's standing, and how hard it lands depends on the mother's [b][color=#d9d2e9]culture[/color][/b].. strict peoples take such a thing gravely, those used to several spouses barely raise an eyebrow. The mother herself reacts in [i]her own character[/i]: a warm-hearted one longs for you to come and see the child; a proud, wounded one wants only that you stop pretending it away; and a cold, scheming one turns the secret into [b][color=#cc0000]leverage[/color][/b].. a letter first, then a conversation, pressing you for coin to keep her silence. Pay her off.. the [b]Give Gold[/b] button settles it.. and you buy a season's peace before she may ask again; refuse, and she may let it slip. And when the child [b]comes of age[/b], it may press a [b]claim[/b] of its own.. a maternal house staking what it feels owed, and a bastard who can grow into a real enemy. What you start in the bedchamber echoes down the years.

[b][color=#e69138]Mature & adult content (opt-in, gated)[/color][/b]
A single setting controls how much romantic and intimate content NPCs may express.. from [color=#dd7e6b][b]Off[/b][/color] (none) through [color=#dd7e6b][b]Mature[/b][/color] (romance, nothing explicit) to [color=#dd7e6b][b]Explicit[/b][/color] and [b][color=#dd7e6b]Hardcore[/color][/b]. Higher tiers unlock orientation and preference discovery, genuine intimacy gated by trust, and.. at Hardcore only.. captor/captive scenes for players who want darker, adult-only roleplay. All of it is [b][color=#dd7e6b]off the explicit path by default[/color][/b] and entirely your choice.

[size=4]Make it your own: editable prompt files[/size]

A lot of what the NPCs "know" and how they carry themselves isn't locked in the code.. it lives in [b][color=#00ff00]plain text and JSON files[/color][/b] shipped with the mod, which get injected into the prompt sent to the AI. Open them in any text editor, change what you like, and the NPCs follow your version on the next conversation. No recompiling, no special tools.

You'll find them in:
`...\Mount & Blade II Bannerlord\Modules\CalradiaRemembers\ModuleData\`

[b][color=#6aa84f]player_description.txt[/color][/b]: optional flavor the game cannot know on its own. Your character's [i]name[/i], [i]gender[/i], and [i]clan[/i] are already pulled from the live game.. NPCs will address you by your real in-game name from the first conversation, with the right pronouns, without any editing needed. What you add here is the color behind those facts: your bearing, how you look, your reputation before you even open your mouth. Ships empty; nothing to fix before playing. If you want NPCs to speak to you as a specific kind of person.. scarred veteran, charming adventurer, feared warrior.. write a paragraph here and they'll carry it.

[b][color=#6aa84f]world.txt[/color][/b]: the shared backdrop of Calradia (its cultures, feudal order, the rhythm of the seasons, the fact that there is no magic). Injected once for every NPC. Edit it to shift the tone or lore of the whole world your characters inhabit.

[b][color=#6aa84f]behavior_guidelines.txt[/color][/b]: [color=#00ff00][i]how[/i][/color] NPCs behave: the dignity of a lord, reading the mood of a conversation, how memory and consequences weigh on them, and faction-by-faction attitudes. This is the dial for the overall "voice" of every character. Tune it to make your Calradia warmer, harsher, more formal, more blunt.. whatever you prefer.

[b][color=#6aa84f]post_history_instructions.txt[/color][/b]: free-form text injected at the [b]very end[/b] of the prompt, right before the model replies.. the highest-recency position, so it carries the strongest steer. If you come from SillyTavern-style setups, this is the equivalent of the [i]post-history instructions / jailbreak[/i] field. Whatever you write here is sent verbatim, with no wrapping from the mod, so it's yours to use for a final tone push, a roleplay framing, or a censorship-bypass preamble. [i]A note on placement:[/i] [b]behavior_guidelines.txt[/b] is your "main system prompt" (early, sets the global voice); [b]post_history_instructions.txt[/b] is your "last word" (read just before the reply). For getting past a model's reluctance, the last-word slot tends to land hardest.. though model choice and the [b]Reasoning Effort[/b] / [b]Adult Content Level[/b] settings still matter most. Ships empty.. nothing changes until you write something in it.

[size=4]Fixing NPC memory (console)[/size]

Occasionally the AI will produce a bad response that gets recorded into an NPC's memory, and future conversations keep building on the mistake. You can fix this directly from the Bannerlord cheat console ([b]Alt + ~[/b], must be enabled in the launcher's engine options).

[b][color=#93c47d]cr.memory [name][/color][/b].. list every recorded event for an NPC with its index:
[code]
cr.memory Liena
  [0] Day 12 | FirstMeeting | We met for the first time in Pen Cannoc.
  [1] Day 45 | Other        | She forgot who she was entirely.   <- this one
  [2] Day 46 | Other        | She spoke to me as a stranger.     <- and this one[/code]

[b][color=#93c47d]cr.memory_add [days_ago] [text] [name][/color][/b].. create a memory dated in the past, to build a prior relationship with a character so not everyone starts as a stranger:
[code]cr.memory_add 30 We fought side by side at Pendraic. Derthert[/code]

[b][color=#93c47d]cr.memory_set [index] [new text] [name][/color][/b].. replace the text of one event without deleting it:
[code]
cr.memory_set 1 She remembered our bond, speaking with warmth. Liena
cr.memory_set 2 She recovered herself and called me by name. Liena[/code]

[b][color=#93c47d]cr.forget [index] [name][/color][/b].. delete one bad event entirely:
[code]cr.forget 1 Liena[/code]

[b][color=#93c47d]cr.forget_all [name][/color][/b].. wipe the entire memory of an NPC for a clean slate:
[code]cr.forget_all Liena[/code]

[quote]💡 The [name] at the end is optional.. if you already have a conversation open with the NPC, you can omit it. The last word of the command is tried as an NPC name; if it matches a character it is used as such, otherwise the whole text after the index is treated as the new memory and the current conversation NPC is used. Remember that NPC memories live inside your campaign save: edits (and everything NPCs record) persist when you [b]save the game[/b], so save after fixing.[/quote]

[size=5]How to configure[/size]

[b]CHOOSE A BACKEND[/b]. The mod supports three backends: [b]OpenRouter[/b] (default, broadest cloud model catalog), [b]NanoGPT[/b] (alternative cloud provider), and [b]OpenAI-Compatible[/b] (any local server on your machine.. LM Studio, Ollama, vLLM). You only need one. Each keeps its own settings, and you switch between them in one click with the "LLM Provider" dropdown.

OPTION A.. SET UP OPENROUTER (CLOUD, ONE TIME)

  1. Create a free account at openrouter.ai
  2. Add a little credit to your account (pay-as-you-go.. you only spend what you use).
  3. Go to openrouter.ai/keys and create an API key. Copy it.
  4. Browse openrouter.ai/models if you'd like to pick a specific model (optional).

[b]HOW FAR DOES CREDIT GO[/b]? Measured on the default model (Grok 4.20): a full week of heavy testing.. 146 exchanges, much of it long, prompt-hungry captive scenes.. came to about $1. That's roughly 130+ messages per dollar, so even $5 covers hundreds of exchanges.. comfortably dozens of full conversations.. and more for everyday chats. You only pay for what you use, and a lighter model stretches it further still.

[quote]🔒 Your key is stored locally on your machine only. Never share your config file or a screenshot of the key field.. treat the key like a password.[/quote]


OPTION B.. SET UP NANOGPT INSTEAD (CLOUD, ONE TIME)

  1. Create an account at nano-gpt.com and add a little credit (also pay-as-you-go).
  2. Create an API key in your NanoGPT account and copy it.
  3. Browse nano-gpt.com/models and copy the exact id of the model you want.. NanoGPT has no default in the mod, so you must paste one (model ids differ from OpenRouter's, so a name from one provider won't work on the other).

[quote]🔒 The same applies: your NanoGPT key is stored locally on this machine only.. treat it like a password.[/quote]


OPTION C.. RUN A LOCAL SERVER (FREE, PRIVATE, NO SUBSCRIPTION)

If you have a capable GPU (or are patient on CPU), you can run the AI entirely on your own machine.. no cloud account, no cost per message, no data leaving your computer.

[i]The OpenAI-Compatible provider also accepts any [b]hosted[/b] OpenAI-style API, not just a local server.. point the Endpoint URL at [b]https://api.openai.com/v1[/b] with your OpenAI key to use OpenAI's own models, including the newer gpt-5 and o-series reasoning models (leave Parameter Compatibility on Auto and they just work).[/i]

[size=4]For modders: make Calradia Remembers your modlist's memory[/size]

This is the part I am most eager to share. Calradia Remembers is not a closed box.. it exposes a small, one-way, validated [b]inbound API[/b] so that [b][color=#ffe599]any other mod can feed events into it[/color][/b], and CR turns them into something its NPCs are aware of.. a chronicle entry, a rumour, hearsay a lord brings up in conversation.. with no further wiring on your side.

Write a mod that changes the world.. a horse race with winners and purses, a plague, a guild war, a festival.. and tell CR about it in a single call:

[code]
CalradiaRemembers.Feed.Record(new ExternalWorldEvent {
    Id        = "myMod_race_42",
    SourceMod = "GrandRaces",
    Summary   = "Dellio of Pravend won the autumn race and a purse of 5000 denars.",
    Magnitude = 40,
    FactionIds = new[] { "empire" },
});[/code]

From that moment, lords who would plausibly have heard speak of it.. as [i]hearsay[/i], hedged by distance and faction, exactly like the news CR records on its own. Your mod needs to know [i]nothing[/i] about CR's memory system, its prompts, or its save format.

Facts can also [b]change[/b]. For things that aren't one-off events but standing state.. a settlement that moves, a character renamed, a place that ceases to exist.. [i]Feed.Update(...)[/i] replaces a previous entry by its id, and [i]Feed.Retract(id)[/i] removes one that is no longer true, so the world's gossip stays current instead of repeating something stale.

It is deliberately [b]one-way and guarded[/b].. you push facts in through one door, and never read or mutate CR's internals. That door validates everything it is handed: it bounds the text, clamps the magnitude, drops heroes or factions it cannot resolve, de-duplicates by id, and rate-limits each source.. so a misbehaving feed can neither bloat a player's save nor flood the gossip. The data contract is a stable, plain-string DTO (no Bannerlord types in it), so it will not break under you as CR evolves.

The result is that CR can act as the [b][color=#e69138]narrative-memory bus[/color][/b] of an entire modlist: every mod that changes the world can give that change a memory and a voice, and the lords of Calradia will talk about it. Openness as a feature.. the opposite of a mod you can only use but never build on.

[b][color=#e69138]…and give the player a mission[/color][/b]
The second door lets your mod set the player a [b]mission[/b], and CR runs the whole ceremony around it:

[code]
CalradiaRemembers.MissionBoard.Register(new ExternalMission {
    Id          = "grandRaces_series_7",
    SourceMod   = "GrandRaces",
    GiverHeroId = "lord_caladog",          // the NPC who frames and rewards it
    Title       = "Win the autumn racing series",
    Description = "Take the series at Pen Cannoc and the purse is yours.",
    RewardGold  = 5000,
    RewardRelation = 5,
    DeadlineDays = 30
});[/code]

CR issues it as a real quest: the giver brings it up in conversation in their own voice, it mirrors into the vanilla journal, and a deadline/abandon/self-heal lifecycle keeps it tidy (no ghost quests, even from a third-party mod). The crucial line is [b]who decides it's done[/b]: YOUR mod owns the completion signal.. it calls [i]MissionBoard.Complete(id)[/i] when its own deed is truly finished (only it can know), and CR pays the gated reward when the player reports back to the giver. CR never pays on the AI's word, and never on yours alone.. the player still has to come collect. You define an arbitrary mission (a tournament series, a smuggling run, surviving a siege) without CR knowing your internals, and without weakening its "rewards only for real deeds" promise.

If you would like to plug a mod into either door, get in touch.. I would love to help.

[size=4]For modders: the NpcMemoryService SDK[/size]

Calradia Remembers is built on top of [b]NpcMemoryService[/b], an open SDK I designed from scratch to wire a large language model into a game so NPCs can hold real conversations and remember them across a whole campaign. The SDK is engine-agnostic (.NET Standard 2.0, no dependency on any game engine or UI toolkit), fully documented, and designed to be extended.

[b]As a player you don't need to install anything extra[/b].. the SDK is bundled inside this mod and works out of the box.

If you are a modder and want to build on this foundation.. add new conversation features, new action types, new memory behaviours, or wire the SDK into a completely different game.. the SDK is available separately here:

[url=https://www.nexusmods.com/mountandblade2bannerlord/mods/11658]NpcMemoryService SDK on Nexus[/url]

It includes full XML documentation on every public type, a runnable reference integration (ConsoleRunner) that shows the complete wiring end-to-end, and a clean architecture you can extend without touching the core. You publish the game actions you want the model to be able to request, implement the verbs yourself, and the SDK handles everything else: memory, prompting, parsing, compression, and persistence. Contributions and extensions are welcome.
