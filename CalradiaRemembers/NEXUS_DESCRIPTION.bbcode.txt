[size=4]Why this mod exists[/size]

Calradia Remembers began as a ground-up answer to a simple wish: an LLM-driven roleplay mod whose code I fully own, understand, and can keep alive.

The idea of talking freely to Bannerlord's characters isn't new.. AI Influence showed the community what was possible. But its source is obfuscated: it can't be studied, fixed, or extended by anyone but its author, and when something doesn't work as expected you're stuck waiting. I didn't want to build on a black box. So I wrote Calradia Remembers from scratch, every line my own, with a clean and documented codebase.. a mod that can be maintained, audited, and grown openly for as long as people want to play it.

What came out of that is, I think, more than a clone. The heart of this mod is [b][color=#ffff00]memory[/color][/b]: your NPCs remember.

..no more "0 conversation" with NPC you already had a conversation with.

Three commitments shape every decision in this project:

[b]1. Honour the modding community.[/b] Modding lives on sharing and respect.. between creators and players, and toward the original game. CR is open by design: readable code, extensible architecture, honest release notes about what is tested and what is not. No black boxes. No hollow promises.

[b]2. Enrich Bannerlord, not replace it.[/b] The goal is not a parallel roleplay world disconnected from the game. NPCs in CR remember, react, and speak.. but they remain lords and ladies of Calradia, not characters in a novel. What they promise lands in the quest journal. The bonds they forge shift the political map. CR amplifies what is already there; it does not override it.

[b]3. Code that deserves the ambition.[/b] A mod that lasts needs architecture that holds. Pure logic isolated from the engine, injected seams, tests that document expected behaviour.. not implementation details. When a behaviour changes unintentionally, a test catches it before a player does.

[line]
[size=4]What it does[/size]

Every notable NPC in Calradia keeps a private, persistent memory of you.. stored inside your save, not a side file. You don't pick lines from a menu: you [i][color=#c9daf8]type whatever you want[/color][/i], and the character answers in their own voice, shaped by who they are, what your two factions are doing, and everything that has passed between you before.

Make an oath and break it, and they'll remember the betrayal months later. Earn a lord's trust through deeds, and his warmth is real. Insult a lady in front of her peers, and word gets around. Conversations leave marks.. on relations, on reputation, sometimes on your purse.. and those marks last!

[line]
[size=4]Features[/size]

[b][color=#e69138]Persistent NPC memory[/color][/b]
Each character records notable events between you.. first meetings, agreements, betrayals, intimate moments, partings.. stamped with the in-game day and carried forward in your save.  When you meet again, they speak as someone who lived through all of it. A built-in, LLM-driven memory compressor keeps long histories from bloating while protecting the moments that matter.

[b][color=#e69138]Free-text conversation, in character[/color][/b]
A clean overlay chat.. no dialogue trees. NPCs reply with the weight of their clan, their liege, and their personality. They judge you by deeds, not flattery, and they don't casually give up their secrets. Like to write long? A small [b]<[/b] button beside the input opens a wide, wrapped, scrollable [b]review panel[/b] so you can read your whole message back before you send it. And a [b]Memories[/b] button opens a side panel that lists, plainly, everything this character remembers of you.. dated, oldest first, like a shared journal, without ever leaving the conversation.

[b][color=#e69138]Woven into the vanilla game[/color][/b]
The mod plugs straight into Bannerlord's own dialogue: walk up to any lord exactly as you always do, and a "[i]We need to talk…[/i]" option sits among the normal choices. Pick it and the character greets you.. with a real vanilla facial expression that matches how they feel about you, warm, neutral, or cold.. and an opening line that itself varies with their regard and character rather than a single fixed phrase.. then the chat opens to continu the conversation with a LLM. Close it and you're back in the ordinary conversation. Nothing native is removed: trade, quests, and every vanilla option are still right where you left them. The mod gives the characters a voice; it doesn't replace your game.

[b][color=#e69138]Living personalities[/color][/b]
Personalities and traits drawn from each hero, relationship awareness (friends, rivals, family, liege), and a sense of standing.. a noble of a great clan does not speak to you the way a landless adventurer does.

[b][color=#e69138]A voice fit to their station[/color][/b]
Not everyone in Calradia carries themselves like a lord. A [b][color=#d5a6bd]gang leader[/color][/b] rules an alley, not a realm.. quiet menace, a knife within reach, no throne to rise from and no guards to wave away. A town [b][color=#d5a6bd]notable[/color][/b] thinks in ledgers and favours; a [b][color=#d5a6bd]wanderer[/color][/b] speaks like the road taught them, unimpressed by titles. Each social station carries itself in its own register instead of every character sounding like a noble on a dais.. and, like the rest of the prompt, it's [i]yours to rewrite[/i]: each register lives in its own file (`behavior_guidelines_gangleader.txt`, `_notable.txt`, `_wanderer.txt`) beside the lordly default.

[b][color=#e69138]Choose the voice your story is told in[/color][/b]
A [b][color=#d5a6bd]quill button[/color][/b] in the chat window opens a picker of [b]narrative styles[/b].. the writing voice the AI answers in. Pick plain [i]Novelistic[/i], fast [i]Terse[/i], or unromantic [i]Grimdark[/i].. or an author-anchored voice: [i]Tolkien, Martin, Cornwell, Austen, McCarthy, Dumas, Kay, Homer, Dostoevsky, Howard[/i], and more. A style changes only [color=#c9daf8]how[/color] the prose reads, never who a character is, what they know, or what they would do.. those are decided elsewhere and always win. Your choice applies everywhere.. lords and commoners alike, on whichever model you run. And like the rest of the prompt, it's [i]yours to rewrite[/i]: each style is a plain text file under `ModuleData\Styles`, so you can tune one or drop in your own.. even mid-game, your next line uses it. (Console: [color=#a4c2f4]cr.styles[/color], [color=#a4c2f4]cr.style <name>[/color].)

[b][color=#e69138]NPCs see what you actually look like[/color][/b]
Every character you speak to perceives your [b]real appearance[/b], read live from your actual character: gender, age, build, hair colour and skin tone (resolved from the game's own face data), plus eye colour. No more NPCs inventing a description that contradicts the person standing in front of them. Add your own touch with [color=#a4c2f4]cr.appearance <text>[/color] ("moves with a slight limp, a scar across the left brow") and every NPC weaves it in. Each NPC keeps their [i]own[/i] appearance straight too.. and even a bandit captor who does not know your name still sees the frame and bearing of the prisoner in front of him.

[b][color=#e69138]Ask about other people[/color][/b]
Mention another character and the NPC answers from their own vantage point.. friend, rival, kin, or stranger. Ask where someone is, and if it is a person they would plausibly know.. a clansman, a close friend, someone of their own faction.. they may tell you, as hearsay: "[i]Last I heard, she was riding toward Ocs Hall.[/i]" A distant stranger they cannot place, and [color=#a4c2f4]no one invents a whereabouts[/color] they were never told.

[b][color=#e69138]Culture matters (and it's yours to shape)[/color][/b]
Calradia's peoples don't all think alike, and the characters reflect it. A Battanian, where women fight, inherit, and lead, meets you very differently from a Vlandian or Aserai lord who expects modesty and deference, while the progressive Southern Empire under Empress Rhagaea takes female authority in stride.  A [b][color=#d5a6bd]female player character[/color][/b] runs into authentic cultural skepticism in the patriarchal factions, not blank generic politeness. All of this ships ready-made with the mod.. and, like the rest of the prompt, it's [i]yours to rewrite[/i]: the cultural rules live in `cultural_traditions.json` and `behavior_guidelines.txt` (see [i][color=#c9daf8]Make it your own[/color][/i] below), so you can soften them, sharpen them, or reshape a whole culture or create a new world however you like.

[b][color=#e69138]Real consequences[/color][/b]
What's said in conversation can change the world: relation shifts, gold given or taken, and [b][color=#b6d7a8]informal quests[/color][/b] an NPC may offer you on their own initiative.. verified by [i][color=#93c47d]actual[/color][/i] game events before any reward is paid. No reward for a story you merely tell; only for a deed genuinely done.

[b][color=#e69138]Grudges and kindnesses.. a memory with two sides[/color][/b]
Beyond a single relation number, an NPC now carries specific, event-anchored feelings about you. A [b][color=#d5a6bd]grudge[/color][/b] is a particular wound.. a warned plot, a broken pledge, a marriage you shattered, a prisoner you sold to their enemy.. that colours how they treat you, cold beneath any courtesy when it runs deep. Someone can like you and still hold one against you (reconcilable), or dislike you and nurse one (far harder to win back). Its mirror is the [b][color=#d5a6bd]kindness[/color][/b]: the gifts, the freedoms, the tasks done on their behalf that they remember fondly, and that a fresh wound can sour. Both are shown, in plain words, on their encyclopedia page, side by side. And a grievance can be [i]mended[/i].. ask what it would take, they name their price in character, and an apology, a worthy gift, or a real deed in their favour eases it or lays it to rest, though a blood vendetta, they will tell you, cannot be undone.

[b][color=#e69138]Join a kingdom as a mercenary through conversation[/color][/b]
A lord who wants reliable swords.. or a player who needs a contract.. can negotiate mercenary service [color=#ffe599]right there in the chat window[/color]. When your clan carries no existing obligation, any lord of a kingdom can offer you a contract on behalf of their realm, and you can propose it first if the mood calls for it. When you both clearly agree, your clan enrolls immediately: no separate menu, no lord's hall required.

[b][color=#e69138]Recruit companions through conversation[/color][/b]
Meet a wanderer in a tavern and convince them to ride with you.. [color=#ffd966]in your own words[/color], not through a menu. The asking price is the same one the vanilla game would charge.. and it is negotiable: impress them and they may come down, lowball them and they will hold firm or walk away. Fine titles do not waive the fee, and dressing the hire up as "joining my clan" will not get you out of paying.. a deal is a deal on the steppe. When you do strike one, they truly join your party, coin changes hands once, and the bargain itself becomes part of your shared history.

[i]New.. pay in goods, not just coin.[/i] Short on denars? Ask whether something else might seal the deal, and a wanderer may take payment in kind. A bag icon in the chat opens a picker of your party inventory.. whole stacks, each shown with its value.. so you can settle the hire with wares instead of money. Goods are reckoned at roughly double the coin price, and a proud or hard-headed character may refuse the barter and hold out for denars, in character. You can also simply [b]give an NPC an item[/b] from your inventory as a gift in any conversation, and watch them react to it. And beside the bag sits a [b]Give Gold[/b] button.. name a sum and hand over coin on the spot, whether as a gift or to settle a lord who asked you to bring them a purse of denars.

[b][color=#e69138]A lord who rides with you.. and the notable who joins your house[/color][/b]
A friendly noble of another clan can agree, in conversation, to [b]accompany you[/b] for a time.. for whatever reason the moment gives: friendship, a shared cause, or a courtship. If they [b]command an army[/b] of their own, their party escorts yours and they keep their command; if they hold no party, they [b]ride in your own party[/b] while staying of their own house and colours, never joining your clan. You are told when one joins or leaves, and if their people go to war with yours they break off to answer that call.. so a companion won today may be an enemy tomorrow. Send them on their way whenever you wish.

And a [b][color=#d5a6bd]notable[/color][/b].. a town's gang leader and the like.. can be won over entirely: earn their deep trust and persuade them to leave their post and swear into your clan as a [b]companion[/b]. They hand their gang and their standing to one of their own people, who takes up the post after them, and they arrive at your side already an ally.

[b][color=#e69138]Quests that actually resolve[/color][/b]
The tasks NPCs hand you aren't throwaway flavor text.. each one is mirrored into Bannerlord's real quest journal, and it always closes cleanly. Complete it (confirmed by [color=#ffe599][i]actual[/i][/color] game events, never by the AI simply saying so), let its deadline lapse, abandon it honestly, or watch its target stop making sense.. every outcome resolves the journal entry, and a self-check on every load force-closes anything left dangling. [color=#f1c232]No ghost quests piling up in your log, and no errand that can never be finished[/color].. a common rough edge when an LLM is bolted onto the vanilla quest system, designed out from the start here. And a task an NPC offers is [b]always a real, doable quest[/b]: before you even speak, the mod checks the living world for what THIS person can truly set you.. a real lair near his lands, a real enemy lord when his realm is at war, a letter to a real kinsman.. and offers only those, with real names. [color=#f1c232]No mission spoken aloud that never lands in your journal[/color], and when you ask for work, there is always something real to be done.

[b][color=#e69138]Bargains beyond coin[/color][/b]
A lord's price is not always denars. One may agree to ride with you only if you [b]hand over an enemy captive[/b].. a rival lord they want in chains: give over one you already hold, or take it as a quest to go capture them and bring them back. Another, with cause against a faction, may make their service conditional on [b]you declaring war[/b] on it.. "rid me of the Vlandians and I am yours". Both are honoured only by the [i][color=#93c47d]real deed[/color][/i].. a true prisoner handed over, a war your own faction actually declares.. never by a promise. The game prices and verifies every such bargain; the talk is yours, the arithmetic is the game's. And the bargain runs both ways.. speak with a [b]captive lord[/b] and they may plead for their freedom: set free one [i]you[/i] hold in exchange for goodwill, gold, or service, or, if another lord holds them, agree to [b]break them out[/b].. a rescue paid only when you truly free them in the field.

[b][color=#e69138]A trade in prisoners.. and bounty hunting[/color][/b]
Captives are a currency you can bargain over, both ways, for gold. A lord may offer to [b]buy[/b] a hero prisoner you are dragging along (a [i]Sell Prisoner[/i] button opens a picker), or [b]sell[/b] you one his own clan holds. You haggle the price in character, but it is held to reason.. anchored to the captive's true ransom worth, so a hard bargain never robs you and a silver tongue never talks a lord into folly. This opens [b]bounty hunting[/b]: a lord at war can set you to bring him an enemy in chains for a price, and a captive you sell into his enemy's hands remembers being handed over.

[b][color=#e69138]Street ambushes and abduction.. when a lord wants you dead[/color][/b]
Cross a lord badly enough.. betray them, humiliate them, put their kin to the sword.. and their hatred can turn murderous. Such a lord may quietly [b][color=#9fc5e8]hire blades to end you[/color][/b], and one day, as you walk into a town, they spring a [b]street ambush[/b]: hooded figures peel from the crowd, steel drawn.. stand and fight, or try to slip away. The band is led by a real soldier of the paymaster's own culture (the tell that a [i]lord[/i], not a street gang, is behind it), and it comes in numbers to match your retinue.. a lone traveller faces a few, a full company a small warband.. while the town's own gate guards scatter from the fray. Any [b]companions[/b] at your side are dragged down and taken with you. Lose the fight and you are [b]abducted[/b]: the band convoys you across the map to the very lord who paid for it.. for a [b]reckoning[/b] face to face.. and holds you captive until ransom, escape, or a [b]rescue[/b] your own people may ride out to mount. It only ever happens if you truly earn someone's murderous hatred; make no such enemy and you will never see it.

[b][color=#e69138]The Nemesis.. the bandit who takes you and does not forget[/color][/b]
When a bandit chief takes you prisoner, he is no longer a faceless nobody you leave behind the moment you are free. The one who holds you becomes a [b]named, persistent figure[/b] the world remembers, who keeps his own account of what passed between you, and whom your road can cross again. And captivity is now a [b]scene you stand inside[/b].. when your captor comes for you, you are brought before them in a proper 3D scene, the two of you face to face, rather than a window over the map. And the rivalry runs deep: he speaks like the coarse, brutal brigand he is rather than a lord, he remembers the day you slip his grasp and throws it in your face, and once you have beaten and taken him prisoner his fate is yours.. put him to death, or spare him and bend the beaten hunter into a sworn man who rides at your own side. Best of all, you can [b]hunt him down[/b]: he keeps no army standing on the map (nothing for a random lord to erase), but ride into his territory to clear bandits and, band after band, a rising chance eventually turns one out to be led by [i]him[/i].. and since he only ever appears in a fight you chose to start, his end is always in your own hands. Kill him and that breed of bandit stays leaderless for a year or three before a new nemesis rises. And once you have crossed him he is no longer a mystery: his [b]encyclopedia page[/b] now keeps your shared history (who he is, the region he ranges, the times you met and how you slipped away), and a lord whose lands he plagues may [b]seek you out[/b] to name him and tell you where he operates, pointing you to the bounty to see him gone, so a nemesis you swore to hunt can actually be found. This rivalry will deepen further in later updates.

[b][color=#e69138]When a companion has given enough[/color][/b]
A companion who fights at your side for years feels the weight of it. Every wound, every time they are taken prisoner on your business, wears them down.. a brave, hardened soul bears far more than a cautious one, and rest between campaigns slowly eases it. When one is truly [b]war-weary[/b] they come to you: a companion with no land of their own asks to [b]retire[/b] from your service, honorably; one who holds a fief or leads a party of their own asks instead to [b]step back from the field[/b] and see to their holdings, while remaining of your clan. So it pays to [b]reward your veterans with land[/b].. a fief keeps them at your side. And drag a companion who has stepped back into war again, and their granted rest curdles into real resentment. When a companion asks for a word.. whether war-weary, or simply unhappy in your service.. you no longer dismiss it with a single click: [b][color=#93c47d]Let us speak of it…[/color][/b] opens a [b]real conversation[/b] where they voice what weighs on them and you answer in your own words.. winning them back, or granting their leave, by what you actually say. Turn them away unheard, and it costs you. (Rides under the [b]Companion contentment[/b] option.)

[b][color=#e69138]Commands and appointments, settled in conversation[/color][/b]
Talk is no longer only talk.. when it genuinely fits your standing, a character acts on what you agree to, then and there, without a menu. Name a trusted companion or vassal the [b][color=#d5a6bd]governor[/color][/b] of one of your fiefs; give a companion a [b]party role[/b].. scout, engineer, quartermaster, or surgeon; call one [b]back into your party[/b], or send one off on an [b]errand[/b].. to bring back word of the realm, spy out a rival, turn a profit at market, or take the measure of a faction; set a companion a standing [b]stipend[/b]; or take a [b][color=#d5a6bd]sworn oath[/color][/b] from someone.. to keep a peace, to protect you, to pay what they owe.. that the world will hold them to. And as a [b]ruler[/b], [b]grant or revoke[/b] a vassal's fief, or [b]cast a companion out[/b] of your clan, all in your own words. Each is offered only when it truly applies to your situation, and the game checks it again before anything actually changes hands.. the conversation proposes, the game decides.

Beyond the holdings, the everyday levers of a great house are yours in conversation too.. [b]lend or grant troops[/b] to a hard-pressed friend, [b]lend political influence[/b] to back a clan at court, [b]sway[/b] a lord's opinion of a third party, [b]arrange a marriage[/b] between two houses, or [b]pledge[/b] to move against a shared rival. As ever, the talk is yours; the arithmetic and the legality are the game's, re-checked before a single denar or soldier moves.

[b][color=#e69138]Your house, remembered across generations[/color][/b]
When your character dies and an heir takes up the banner, Calradia does not forget. The lords you dealt with [color=#6fa8dc][b]inherit[/b] [/color]their standing toward your house.. the debts, the alliances, the grudges.. and speak to your heir of what came before: "[i]your father helped me at the siege of Epicrotea.. I owe your house that debt[/i]," or "[i]your mother's broken word still stings[/i]." The private history of the dead stays buried with them; your heir inherits a reputation, not a diary, and the bond with each lord begins anew from there. Your dynasty carries its name forward.

[b][color=#e69138]Multi-character scenes[/color][/b]
Other lords present in the same hall can overhear, react in their own voice, and take sides.. and who is present actually makes sense: the smith from the market square will not be attending your audience in the keep. Bring up to two of your companions to a meeting (those walking beside you in town join naturally), or go alone. Everything is handled in the conversation itself: address a specific witness directly, ask one of your companions to leave you alone with the NPC in your own words, or request a private audience.. and the NPC decides whether to clear the room. And those who stood in on the talk [b][color=#6fa8dc]remember it[/color][/b]: speak with one of them later, alone, and they recall that they were there and what passed between you.

[b][color=#e69138]Hold a council[/color][/b]
Gather your people to talk as a group, not one at a time. From a [b]new button on the campaign bar[/b] (bottom left), on land, call together your [b]companions[/b], your [b]kin[/b], your [b]clan's lords[/b], or a [b]war council[/b] of your allies.. the button stays within reach whether you are out on the map or inside a town, castle, or village. Everyone sits at one table and speaks in the same scene, each in their own voice, weighing their regard for you, their culture, and their [b]age[/b].. a young one speaks as a young one, an elder with the weight of years. A [b][color=#d5a6bd]book-styled council window[/color][/b] shows who is seated, where the sitting is held, and the decisions the table settles on, and the scene art follows the kind of council and where you hold it. A family council seats your kin old enough to counsel (thirteen and up). Turn the whole feature on or off in the mod options.

[b][color=#e69138]Talk to the locals[/color][/b]
Walk up to any townsperson, villager, or castle servant.. anyone without a title.. and ask what's going on. A short LLM conversation opens with a commoner whose name and trade come from the local culture. [color=#ffd966]What they know is what the street knows[/color]: who rules the settlement, how times have been, which lords are passing through, recent deaths and captures, and the mood of the market. No memory is kept between conversations.. every encounter is a fresh face. The only way coin changes hands is if they think a piece of news is worth paying for.

[b][color=#e69138]A world with news.. and NPCs who've heard it[/color][/b]
Battles, sieges, towns changing hands, noble marriages, executions, declarations of war.. all of it is quietly recorded as it happens, and [color=#b6d7a8]reaches people the way news really travels[/color]. Talk to a lord and they may bring up recent events they would plausibly know of.. what their own realm is caught up in, what happened near them, what is still fresh.. spoken as [i]hearsay[/i], never as omniscience. A Sturgian lord will not recite yesterday's distant Aserai gossip; what reaches someone depends on distance, faction, and how recent it is. And the further word has had to travel, the [i]less sure[/i] it arrives.. a faraway rumour comes wrapped in real doubt ("[i]they say, though none here can vouch for it..[/i]"), and the teller may have a detail wrong, while news from a lord's own region or realm is told plainly. The townsfolk's street rumours are drawn from real events too. And the lords who fought beside you.. or against you.. simply remember how you acquitted yourself, instead of waiting for you to boast of it. The world also speaks of [b][color=#ffe599]you[/color][/b]: a lord who was nowhere near, but to whom word has carried your deeds, may bring them up to your face.. attributed to you, hedged as hearsay.. and they greet you accordingly. Storm a town or put a rival's kin to death and lords of the wounded realm meet you with wariness or fear; do their side a service and it earns you respect. You feel your reputation arrive in the room before you speak.

Word of [color=#d5a6bd][b]disgrace[/b] [/color]travels the same way: affront a lord steeply in front of witnesses and the shame itself becomes a rumour others may have heard, rather than evaporating the moment you part. And when two lords of the same crown nurse real bad blood, word of their [b]rivalry[/b] circulates as ambient gossip.. so a lord may volunteer which of their own peers are at each other's throats.

[b][color=#e69138]Court intrigue.. plots between lords[/color][/b]
The nobility of Calradia does not only feud in the open. A lord may quietly set a [b]scheme[/b] in motion against a rival they cannot abide.. to [b][color=#d5a6bd]blacken their name[/color][/b] until their peers turn cold, or to [b][color=#d5a6bd]undermine their standing[/color][/b] and bleed away their influence. It unfolds on its own, in secret.. the plot gains ground over the weeks and either ripens into real harm to its target, or is caught and collapses before it can land.

You can take a hand in it. A plotter who trusts you may sound you out in conversation and invite you to help.. and if you throw in with them, their scheme leaps forward. But complicity carries a price: should the affair ever be exposed, or succeed with your hand in it, the wronged party comes to know.. and turns on you. The betrayal cuts deeper when you moved against your own realm or a friend than against a stranger or a standing enemy.

Or you can [b]betray the plotter's confidence[/b]. Seek out the lord the plot is [i]aimed at[/i] and warn them.. they hold no certain knowledge of it themselves, and act only on a credible word from you. Forewarned, they drag the conspiracy into the open and stand a real chance of undoing it. A grateful target becomes a genuine [color=#93c47d]ally[/color]; the exposed plotter, suspecting your hand, a solid [color=#cc0000]enemy[/color].. and how warmly the one regards you and how bitterly the other resents you turns on the kind of people they are.. an honourable lord feels a true debt, a cold and calculating one suspects your motive; a vindictive plotter hates you the harder, a merciful one bears it with more grace.

And every plot that comes to light ripples outward as [i]rumour[/i].. the realm's intrigues become something its people hear of, and gossip about, like everything else.

[b][color=#e69138]Settle it with steel.. and the reckoning after[/color][/b]
When words fail, a quarrel of honour can be put to the sword. Call a peer out.. or take up the challenge one throws at you.. and the two of you meet in a real, [b]non-lethal duel[/b]: the loser is beaten senseless, never killed. A character weighs whether to answer from their own honour, valour, and nerve, so a coward, or a lord called out by some nobody far beneath his station, may decline.. at a cost to his standing. And the fight is not the end of it: [b][color=#d5a6bd]afterwards the conversation reopens[/color][/b], right where it broke off before the blades came out. Win, and the beaten party concedes.. and, if beaten honourably, is inclined to grant a reasonable [b]favour[/b] you ask of them ("[i]keep away from her, from now on[/i]", to let a matter drop) and to hold to their word. Lose, and they speak as the victor and make plain what they now expect of you. Either way a defeat costs the loser's house some standing, the more so before a crowd. (You can turn the after-duel conversation off in the mod options.)

[b][color=#e69138]Your Chronicle[/color][/b]
Your own deeds are written into a chronicle in the voice of a medieval chronicler.. "[i]In the autumn of the sixth year, the lord {you} met {foe} in open battle near {place}, and by sword and valour put the foe to flight.[/i]" Open it any time from the [b][color=#d5a6bd]Read the Chronicle[/color][/b] button on your own character's Encyclopedia page: a parchment-styled window of your saga, dated and in order. Your legend, written as you live it.

And if you want more than plain entries, an [b][color=#d5a6bd]Illuminate[/color][/b] button in that window has the AI rewrite your whole saga into flowing, period-voiced chronicler's prose.. and it grows with you, weaving each new deed in as it happens rather than starting over. The voice itself is yours to shape through an editable style file (`chronicle_style.txt`), so your chronicle can read as solemn, florid, or terse as you like.

[color=#e69138][b]Letters & visible couriers[/b][/color]
Correspondence has a home of its own now: reading a letter opens a [b][color=#d5a6bd]two-page leather book[/color][/b]. The whole correspondence with that person runs down the left page (every letter that has ever passed between you, oldest to newest), the letter in your hand sits on the right, and their portrait rides the page.. a custom chat portrait if you have dropped one in, otherwise their usual face. Read a letter the moment it arrives, write a fresh one, or pen a reply.. then hire a courier who physically crosses the campaign map to carry it.. and who can be intercepted by bandits along the way. NPCs write back, and their replies arrive by courier too, read on the same page. A reply is sealed and sent at once.. you compose it in your own words, and the answer finds its way back to you in time.

[i]New.. guard your letters against the road.[/i] A courier carried a long way, when you are far from the sender, used to be waylaid too often. Interception no longer piles up with distance, so ordinary letters get through far more reliably. And when a letter truly matters, you can pay for an [b][color=#d5a6bd]armed escort[/color][/b] at the moment you hire the courier: that whole correspondence, both directions and every reply after it, then rides guarded and always arrives, safe from bandits. If you would rather never lose one at all, a single [b]Invulnerable couriers[/b] switch in the mod options turns interception off entirely.

[i]New.. a lost letter can betray you (off by default).[/i] Turn on [b]Intercepted letters can leak[/b] in the mod options and a letter waylaid on the road may fall into the wrong hands.. and someone who wishes you ill may write to you about what they read.

[i]New.. every letter is kept, and now always in view.[/i] Your correspondence is never lost and never trimmed: the full dated thread with a person, oldest first, each letter headed with the season and day it was sent, sits open on the left page every time you read one of their letters. You can still open it on its own from a character's encyclopedia page with the [b][color=#d5a6bd]Letters[/color][/b] button.

[color=#e69138][b]Encyclopedia integration[/b][/color]
What you learn about a character surfaces on their own encyclopedia page. Alongside their standing toward you, a "[b][color=#d5a6bd]Discoveries[/color][/b]" panel fills in over time: as conversations turn personal, NPCs let slip who they really are.. their temperament, their romantic nature, their preferences, and (with adult content enabled) the more intimate traits and [color=#ff00ff][b]kinks[/b][/color] they reveal. Nothing is handed to you on a plate.. you earn this portrait through trust and talk, one character at a time, and each thing learned is written into their Encyclopedia page for good.

[color=#e69138][b]Story Log (optional)[/b][/color]
Turn it on (in options) and every conversation and letter is written out as a clean, novel-style transcript.. no timestamps, no technical noise.. so you can re-read your campaign like a book.

[b][color=#e69138]Romance & courtship that emerge on their own[/color][/b]
This isn't only an adult feature. From the [color=#8e7cc3][b]Mature[/b][/color] setting up, attraction grows with trust, and an unmarried lord or lady may raise courtship.. or propose marriage outright.. on their [color=#d9d2e9][i]own[/i][/color] initiative when your standing and your shared history warrant it: lead a clan of real rank, or make a name in the tournament lists, and the right people start to notice. You can propose just as freely. How far things go is governed by the Adult Content Level below; that they happen at all is simply Calradia's characters reacting to who you've become.

[b][color=#e69138]Marriage.. family politics, love matches, and letters of proposal[/color][/b]
A match joins two houses, and the house gets a say. Ask the head of a family for the hand of their kin and they weigh your worth against their name.. granting their blessing freely, refusing it, or setting a single worthy deed as its price. Wed into a house [i]without[/i] that blessing and its members take the slight personally and cool toward you; earn it, and the family embraces the union.. though a dissenting cousin may still quietly resent it. Love and ambition, pulling against each other.

When the bond between you and someone runs deep enough.. and you have both earned it.. marriage can also be proposed [i]in conversation[/i] itself: by you, by them, or by both arriving at it together. When you agree, it is real: the game's full marriage takes effect.

Lords also write first. A clan head who regards you well may send a courier proposing a match with one of their eligible kin before you have ever thought to ask. The door can open from their side.

[i]New.. a courtship put to the test.[/i] Some will not be won by words alone. Court someone and have them [b]ride at your side[/b] (see above) for a season or two, and the time spent together settles them: they [b]write to you themselves[/b] to accept the match, and the marriage opens. The trial only clears the "not close enough yet" reluctance.. never a real barrier such as an existing marriage, a war between your houses, or kinship.

[i]New.. marry one of your own companions.[/i] The person you have campaigned beside for years need not be a born noble to become your spouse. Win a wanderer you recruited into your clan, and.. once your regard is high enough and neither of you is otherwise wed.. you can [b]wed them[/b]: they are raised to a lordship of your house and the marriage takes hold like any other. Blood relatives remain firmly off-limits, and no match is arranged while either of you is caught up in an army. Should the marriage ever end, a former companion-spouse, having no birth house to return to, rejoins the pool of wanderers you might one day recruit again, rather than lingering as a stranded noble.

[b][color=#e69138]Consort bonds[/color][/b]
Not every deep commitment needs to be.. or can be.. a legal marriage. When a relationship has grown genuinely intimate, both parties can name it openly: a consort bond is a private, mutual pledge that both of you honour, even though Calradian law does not recognise it. It carries no clan merger, no inheritance claim.. but it is real, and your consort will write to you as a spouse would when you have been apart too long. This path is open even if you are already wed.

[b][color=#e69138]Jealousy.. romance with rivals[/color][/b]
Love is no longer without risk. Court, kiss, or wed someone and a third party with a real stake in them may turn against you.. never a random grudge, always someone with a believable reason: the wronged spouse of the one you courted, your own slighted partner, a close associate who had hoped to marry them first, or an admirer of yours you passed over. They find out the way people really do.. if the moment had [b]witnesses[/b], word travels fast, so flirting with a married lady in front of her kin is a risk you can feel coming. And a [i]private[/i] affair is no longer safe either.. word of it seeps out through the same rumour network and may reach them later all the same, so discretion and distance only buy you time. Days later the consequence lands: their regard for you cools, they remember the slight, and they may send a [b][color=#d5a6bd]letter[/color][/b].. a cold warning, a wounded reproach, or an open threat.. "[i]leave her be.. I mean to marry her one day[/i]."

And it reads the [b][color=#d9d2e9]culture[/color][/b] of the wronged party. Among strictly monogamous peoples a love given elsewhere is a betrayal; among cultures that accept several spouses.. the steppe Khuzait, the Aserai, the highborn of Battania.. no one is outraged that you took another, but now your partners compete for standing and attention, and a great love becomes a harder one to keep. Which peoples hold which view lives in an editable `jealousy_cultures.json`, so a total-conversion or faction mod can set its own customs without touching the code.

[b][color=#e69138]Open relationships.. a freedom that runs both ways[/color][/b]
Some couples come to an understanding. You and a committed partner.. a spouse or a consort.. can agree to [b][color=#d5a6bd]open terms[/color][/b], and the offer may come from either side: you can propose that each of you is free to love another, or she may raise it herself.. asking your leave to take a lover, or urging you to wed another. Once it is settled, an open partner no longer resents your other loves. But the freedom is [i]hers too[/i]: in time she takes a lover of her own, the more readily the more you have left her wanting for attention, and she meets him while you are away. You may come to hear of it.. an open love is spoken of plainly, and you can raise it with her, ask her to [b]end the affair[/b], or [b]revoke the open terms[/b] altogether. In a [b][color=#d9d2e9]harem-minded culture[/color][/b] such as the Aserai, a wife does not flaunt it: her affair stays [b]secret[/b], and it may one day surface as a [b]scandal[/b] you were the last to know. And a child she conceives by her lover belongs to [b]his[/b] line and hers, never yours. If none of this is the story you want, a single [b]option[/b] turns the whole jealousy and competition system off for a drama-free household.

[b][color=#e69138]Stolen love.. win a heart already given (off by default)[/color][/b]
Some loves are not free to take. Turn on [b]Stolen Love[/b] in the mod options and you may set out to win away a [b][color=#d5a6bd]married[/color][/b] character. It is no easy conquest: whether they can be tempted at all is gated by [i]who they are[/i].. an honourable soul, or one contentedly wed, holds firm however high your standing, while a colder or unhappier heart is open to persuasion. Court them, and over the weeks their marriage [b]frays[/b]; in time they resolve to [b]leave their spouse[/b], and once the divorce is through you may [b]marry them yourself[/b].. the groundwork already laid, so it asks no fresh courtship.. or simply leave them divorced and walk away. Either way the [b][color=#cc0000]jilted spouse[/color][/b] does not forget who unmade their marriage, and carries a personal grudge for it. Explicit content still follows your Adult Content Level; the path itself stays off unless you choose it.

[b][color=#e69138]Intimacy can beget a child[/color][/b]
An intimate scene carried through to its end can leave you with more than a memory: a real pregnancy. It runs both ways.. the mother may be your own character or the NPC.. and consensual scenes count just as captive ones do. It is a chance, never a certainty, and it obeys the ordinary rules of the world.. a woman of bearing age, not already carrying and not freshly delivered. The child follows the [b]mother's clan[/b]. The mod stays quiet about it, the way the game does, so you find out the vanilla way.. the pregnant status on her page, and in time the birth. It needs Adult Content set to [b]Explicit[/b] or higher, and the scene to actually reach its conclusion rather than fade out early.

[b][color=#e69138]A child kept secret.. and the years it takes to come back[/color][/b]
Romance can leave more than memories. A liaison may quietly produce a [b][color=#d5a6bd]hidden child[/color][/b].. born out of wedlock, the realm only guessing at its father.. and that secret does not stay buried forever. Season by season there is a growing chance it surfaces.. the older the child, the likelier the day.. and when it does, a [b]scandal[/b] breaks: a blow to your renown and your clan's standing, and how hard it lands depends on the mother's [b][color=#d9d2e9]culture[/color][/b].. strict peoples take such a thing gravely, those used to several spouses barely raise an eyebrow. The mother herself reacts in [i]her own character[/i]: a warm-hearted one longs for you to come and see the child; a proud, wounded one wants only that you stop pretending it away; and a cold, scheming one turns the secret into [b][color=#cc0000]leverage[/color][/b].. a letter first, then a conversation, pressing you for coin to keep her silence. Pay her off.. the [b]Give Gold[/b] button settles it.. and you buy a season's peace before she may ask again; refuse, and she may let it slip. And when the child [b]comes of age[/b], it may press a [b]claim[/b] of its own.. a maternal house staking what it feels owed, and a bastard who can grow into a real enemy. What you start in the bedchamber echoes down the years.

[b][color=#e69138]Mature & adult content (opt-in, gated)[/color][/b]
A single setting controls how much romantic and intimate content NPCs may express.. from [color=#dd7e6b][b]Off[/b][/color] (none) through [color=#dd7e6b][b]Mature[/b][/color] (romance, nothing explicit) to [color=#dd7e6b][b]Explicit[/b][/color] and [b][color=#dd7e6b]Hardcore[/color][/b]. Higher tiers unlock orientation and preference discovery, genuine intimacy gated by trust, and.. at Hardcore only.. captor/captive scenes for players who want darker, adult-only roleplay. At Hardcore, captivity alongside your companions carries its own dread: the captor may drag [b]one of them[/b] before you instead.. tormented in person, given to the soldiers, or your own hands forced to it.. and it leaves marks: your companion's trust in you is scarred, the atrocity enters the world's memory and your chronicle, and against a named captor the companion swears a [b]revenge of their own[/b], joining your vendetta. All of it is [b][color=#dd7e6b]off the explicit path by default[/color][/b] and entirely your choice.

[size=4]Make it your own: editable prompt files[/size]

A lot of what the NPCs "know" and how they carry themselves isn't locked in the code.. it lives in [b][color=#00ff00]plain text and JSON files[/color][/b] shipped with the mod, which get injected into the prompt sent to the AI. Open them in any text editor, change what you like, and the NPCs follow your version on the next conversation. No recompiling, no special tools.

You'll find them in:
`...\Mount & Blade II Bannerlord\Modules\CalradiaRemembers\ModuleData\`

[b][color=#6aa84f]Keep your edits across updates.. the Custom folder[/color][/b]: edit a file in ModuleData directly and the next mod update overwrites it with the shipped default. To keep your version, drop a copy into the [b]Custom[/b] subfolder ([b]ModuleData\Custom\[/b]).. same file name, or a style under [b]Custom\Styles\[/b].. and the mod loads yours first and never touches it on update. A short README inside explains it. [b]cultural_traditions.json[/b] and [b]jealousy_cultures.json[/b] can live in Custom too, so an overhaul mod's own custom cultures survive an update just the same.

[b][color=#6aa84f]Template variables ({{char}}, {{user}}..)[/color][/b]: any of the files below, and your narrative styles, can use SillyTavern-style [b]{{token}}[/b] variables. They are filled in fresh for the character you are actually talking to and the hero you actually are, at the moment each reply is written.. so one file writes correctly for every character instead of naming whoever you first used it with. Available: [b]{{char}}[/b] (the character's name), [b]{{char_gender}}[/b], [b]{{user}}[/b] (your hero's name), [b]{{user_gender}}[/b], [b]{{user_clan}}[/b], [b]{{faction}}[/b] (your kingdom, or your own clan if you have none.. also [b]{{user_faction}}[/b]), [b]{{location}}[/b] (the settlement or scene you are in, empty out on the world map), [b]{{spouse}}[/b], [b]{{father}}[/b], [b]{{mother}}[/b]. Case does not matter ([b]{{Char}}[/b] works too), spaces inside the braces are fine, and an unknown token (a typo) is left exactly as written instead of vanishing, so mistakes are easy to spot.

[b][color=#6aa84f]player_description.txt[/color][/b]: optional flavor the game cannot know on its own. Your character's [i]name[/i], [i]gender[/i], and [i]clan[/i] are already pulled from the live game.. NPCs will address you by your real in-game name from the first conversation, with the right pronouns, without any editing needed. What you add here is the color behind those facts: your bearing, how you look, your reputation before you even open your mouth. Ships empty; nothing to fix before playing. If you want NPCs to speak to you as a specific kind of person.. scarred veteran, charming adventurer, feared warrior.. write a paragraph here and they'll carry it.

[b][color=#6aa84f]world.txt[/color][/b]: the shared backdrop of Calradia (its cultures, feudal order, the rhythm of the seasons, the fact that there is no magic). Injected once for every NPC. Edit it to shift the tone or lore of the whole world your characters inhabit.

[b][color=#6aa84f]behavior_guidelines.txt[/color][/b]: [color=#00ff00][i]how[/i][/color] NPCs behave: the dignity of a lord, reading the mood of a conversation, how memory and consequences weigh on them, and faction-by-faction attitudes. This is the dial for the overall "voice" of every character. Tune it to make your Calradia warmer, harsher, more formal, more blunt.. whatever you prefer.

[b][color=#6aa84f]post_history_instructions.txt[/color][/b]: free-form text injected at the [b]very end[/b] of the prompt, right before the model replies.. the highest-recency position, so it carries the strongest steer. If you come from SillyTavern-style setups, this is the equivalent of the [i]post-history instructions / jailbreak[/i] field. Whatever you write here is sent verbatim, with no wrapping from the mod, so it's yours to use for a final tone push, a roleplay framing, or a censorship-bypass preamble. [i]A note on placement:[/i] [b]behavior_guidelines.txt[/b] is your "main system prompt" (early, sets the global voice); [b]post_history_instructions.txt[/b] is your "last word" (read just before the reply). For getting past a model's reluctance, the last-word slot tends to land hardest.. though model choice and the [b]Reasoning Effort[/b] / [b]Adult Content Level[/b] settings still matter most. Ships empty.. nothing changes until you write something in it.

[b][color=#6aa84f]cultural_traditions.json[/color][/b]: the cultural norms of each people (Vlandian, Battanian, Aserai, Sturgian, Khuzait, Imperial… or your own factions): their views on authority, honor, courtship, and gender roles. This is what gives a Battanian and an Aserai noble genuinely different outlooks. Edit the text inside the quotes to reshape a culture.. just keep the JSON structure intact (matching `"Faction": "description"` pairs, commas between them).

[b][color=#6aa84f]character_overrides.json[/color][/b]: hand-author individual NPCs. Give a named character your own five personality traits (valor, honor, mercy, generosity, calculating, each -2..+2) and an optional backstory, and they become that person.. the authored traits are the real source of truth, so they shape how the character speaks, their derived nature, and even the campaign AI. You can also set a character's [b]sexual orientation[/b] here.. add [b][color=#ffe599]"orientation": "bisexual"[/color][/b] (or "lesbian", "gay", "pan", "asexual", "straight") to their entry to enable, say, a female-female romance the default roll would have blocked. It applies live, even to a character already married, and there is a quick [b][color=#93c47d]cr.orientation <value> [name][/color][/b] console command to try it on the spot. Enable authored data with the [b]Use custom traits[/b] option (Mod Options → Calradia Remembers → Character); turn it off to restore vanilla traits. Ships empty.. nothing changes until you add an entry and opt in.

[spoiler]
Each trait runs [b]−2…+2[/b]. Traits set the [i]pool[/i] of a character's leanings; a few are then drawn from it, so a trait biases the [i]range[/i] of who they are rather than forcing one fixed result. The romantic / intimate effects below surface only at the higher adult tiers.

[b][color=#ffe599]Valor[/color][/b].. High: bold, pursues directly, impatient with hesitation, confident. Low: cautious, slow to commit, needs safety first.
[b][color=#ffe599]Honor[/color][/b].. High: keeps their word, strict monogamy, devoted. Low: oaths sit lightly, flexible / casual / open.
[b][color=#ffe599]Mercy[/color][/b].. High: warm, forgiving, attentive, leans submissive / devoted. Low: cold, demanding, no sentiment, leans dominant.
[b][color=#ffe599]Generosity[/color][/b].. High: gives time and resource freely, devoted. Low: holds back, independent, possessive.
[b][color=#ffe599]Calculating[/color][/b].. High: deliberate, observant, slow-burn, reserved. Low: acts on feeling, in-the-moment, intense.

[b]Notable combinations:[/b]
[list]
[*][b]Cynical exploiter[/b] (can proposition a female player at Explicit+): Honor ≤ 0 [b]and[/b] (Calculating ≥ +1 [b]or[/b] Mercy ≤ 0).[/*]
[*][b]Cruel, domineering lord[/b]: Mercy −2, Honor −1, Calculating +2.[/*]

[size=4]Fixing NPC memory (console)[/size]

Occasionally the AI will produce a bad response that gets recorded into an NPC's memory, and future conversations keep building on the mistake. You can fix this directly from the Bannerlord cheat console ([b]Alt + ~[/b], must be enabled in the launcher's engine options).

[b][color=#93c47d]cr.memory [name][/color][/b].. list every recorded event for an NPC with its index:
[code]
cr.memory Liena
  [0] Day 12 | FirstMeeting | We met for the first time in Pen Cannoc.
  [1] Day 45 | Other        | She forgot who she was entirely.   <- this one
  [2] Day 46 | Other        | She spoke to me as a stranger.     <- and this one[/code]

[b][color=#93c47d]cr.memory_add [days_ago] [text] [name][/color][/b].. create a memory dated in the past, to build a prior relationship with a character so not everyone starts as a stranger:
[code]cr.memory_add 30 We fought side by side at Pendraic. Derthert[/code]

[b][color=#93c47d]cr.memory_set [index] [new text] [name][/color][/b].. replace the text of one event without deleting it:
[code]
cr.memory_set 1 She remembered our bond, speaking with warmth. Liena
cr.memory_set 2 She recovered herself and called me by name. Liena[/code]

[b][color=#93c47d]cr.forget [index] [name][/color][/b].. delete one bad event entirely:
[code]cr.forget 1 Liena[/code]

[b][color=#93c47d]cr.forget_all [name][/color][/b].. wipe the entire memory of an NPC for a clean slate:
[code]cr.forget_all Liena[/code]

[quote]💡 The [name] at the end is optional.. if you already have a conversation open with the NPC, you can omit it. The last word of the command is tried as an NPC name; if it matches a character it is used as such, otherwise the whole text after the index is treated as the new memory and the current conversation NPC is used. Remember that NPC memories live inside your campaign save: edits (and everything NPCs record) persist when you [b]save the game[/b], so save after fixing.[/quote]

[b][color=#93c47d]cr.world_event [what happened][/color][/b].. tell the realm something happened and let your NPCs gossip about it:
[code]cr.world_event A great fire gutted the Vlandian docks at Sargot.[/code]
Name a kingdom in the text and its people are likeliest to have heard it. This is a [i]rumour[/i], so it spreads by the same hearsay rules as real news (an NPC brings it up only if they'd plausibly have heard it, as hearsay, when it fits) rather than everyone knowing it instantly. To make ONE specific NPC know a fact directly, use [b]cr.memory_add[/b] above.

[quote]💡 A note on the console: most of the [b]cr.[/b] commands you'll see are developer / regression tests, [b]not gameplay features[/b] (running one won't break anything, but it isn't how you play). The genuinely player-useful ones are the memory commands above, [b]cr.world_event[/b], and the gold/relation cheats. The mod ships a full [b]CONSOLE_COMMANDS_GUIDE.md[/b] (in the CalradiaRemembers module folder) that sorts every command into "cheats & player tools" vs "developer tests", with examples.. and in game, [b]cr.help[/b] lists them all.[/quote]

[size=4]Repairing a character who learned the wrong things about itself[/size]

[b]Who needs this:[/b] anyone who has seen, on a character's encyclopedia page or in the in-chat panel, a "discovery" that describes YOU rather than the character. If you have never seen one, you have nothing to fix.

[b]Why it is worth five minutes.[/b] Those notes are not only shown to you. They are read back to the character at the start of every later conversation, under the heading of what you already know about them. So a note that actually describes YOU is handed to the character as a fact about ITSELF, and it keeps building on that false picture until the note is gone. Updating the mod stops new ones being written. It cannot remove what is already saved in your campaign.

Step 1. Open the console

Press [b]Alt + `[/b] (the key above Tab). Type a command, press Enter.

[code]cr.help[/code]

lists everything the mod offers, if you want to look around.

Step 2. See what a character has recorded

[code]cr.discoveries Ira[/code]

Each line comes with an index in brackets, its key, and its text. Read them and pick out the ones that describe YOU instead of the character.

[quote]💡 If you already have a conversation open with that character, you can leave the name out entirely.[/quote]

Step 3. Remove the wrong ones

[code]cr.discover_forget 2 Ira[/code]

[b]Work from the HIGHEST index down to the lowest.[/b] Removing an entry renumbers everything after it, so deleting index 0 first shifts the rest up by one and your next command hits the wrong line. Run [color=#93c47d]cr.discoveries[/color] again any time you are unsure.

To wipe them all and start clean instead:

[code]cr.discover_forget_all Ira[/code]

This removes the good ones too. The character will simply rediscover things about itself as you talk again, so it costs you nothing but a little time.

Step 4. Save the game

This is the step people miss. Everything characters remember lives inside your save file, so a repair you do not save is a repair you did not make.

How do I know it worked?

Run [color=#93c47d]cr.discoveries Ira[/color] once more, or open her encyclopedia page. What remains should read as things about HER, in the third person, for example [i]"She seems drawn to plain speaking."[/i] Anything describing you does not belong there.

One came back

The guard added in v1.31.7 catches the clearest cases, but it is deliberately cautious: it would rather let an odd line through than silently delete a real discovery you earned. If one slips past, remove it the same way, and please post the [b]exact text[/b] on the bug tab. The wording is what lets me tighten it.

[size=4]Which model should I use?[/size]

The mod works with any text model your provider offers.. you just paste its id into the PROSE model field, and you can switch anytime in one click. Two things matter for this mod: the model must follow instructions reliably (it quietly drives memory, actions, and quests behind the scenes), and.. if you play the Explicit / Hardcore tiers.. it must be permissive, because heavily-filtered models will flat-out refuse adult scenes. You no longer have to pick one model that does both: on the default [b]Enhanced[/b] composition a stronger writer carries the [b]PROSE[/b], while a more permissive [b]NSFW model[/b] quietly finishes any explicit beat that writer would dodge or cut short (see "How to configure" above). The ids below are OpenRouter ids; on NanoGPT the same model families are available but use NanoGPT's own ids from nano-gpt.com/models. Prices below are a rough guide; check your provider's model page for current rates.

[b]GROK 4.20[/b].. the safe default    (model id: x-ai/grok-4.20)
Proven with everything this mod does, adult tiers included. Balanced quality, permissive, and already tested. If in doubt, stay here.

[b]DEEPSEEK V3.2[/b].. ⭐ best value    (model id: deepseek/deepseek-v3.2)
Comparable quality for a fraction of the cost (~5× cheaper input and output). Strong instruction-following, widely regarded as permissive.. the pick to get the same experience for much less.

[b]DEEPSEEK V4 PRO[/b].. premium, still cheap    (model id: deepseek/deepseek-v4-pro)
Top-tier quality, still well under Grok's price. For when you want the very best and don't mind paying a little more than V3.2.

[b]WHAT IT COSTS, BY MODEL[/b]. Only Grok 4.20 is measured from real usage (the ~130 messages-per-dollar figure earlier); the others are extrapolated by price ratio, so treat them as ballpark. For the same play: DeepSeek V3.2 is about 5-6× cheaper than Grok 4.20, and DeepSeek V4 Pro about 3× cheaper. Concretely, the ~$1 week of heavy testing on Grok 4.20 would be roughly ~$0.18 on V3.2 or ~$0.38 on V4 Pro.. your exact cost still depends on conversation length and each model's own caching.

[b]AVOID FOR ADULT PLAY[/b]: mainstream Claude and GPT models are superb writers but refuse Explicit / Hardcore content.. perfectly fine at Off / Mature, frustrating beyond it.

[b]LAST RESORT[/b]: if a mainstream model balks at a scene you want, dedicated uncensored fine-tunes (Dolphin, Cydonia and similar) refuse almost nothing.. but they follow the mod's structured format less reliably and can drift over long scenes, so reach for them only if you have to.

[i]New here? Start on the default (Grok 4.20) or DeepSeek V3.2, play a few conversations, and only tinker with models once you know what you want from them.[/i]

[size=4]For modders: make Calradia Remembers your modlist's memory[/size]

This is the part I am most eager to share. Calradia Remembers is not a closed box.. it exposes a small, one-way, validated [b]inbound API[/b] so that [b][color=#ffe599]any other mod can feed events into it[/color][/b], and CR turns them into something its NPCs are aware of.. a chronicle entry, a rumour, hearsay a lord brings up in conversation.. with no further wiring on your side.

Write a mod that changes the world.. a horse race with winners and purses, a plague, a guild war, a festival.. and tell CR about it in a single call:

[code]
CalradiaRemembers.Feed.Record(new ExternalWorldEvent {
    Id        = "myMod_race_42",
    SourceMod = "GrandRaces",
    Summary   = "Dellio of Pravend won the autumn race and a purse of 5000 denars.",
    Magnitude = 40,
    FactionIds = new[] { "empire" },
});[/code]

From that moment, lords who would plausibly have heard speak of it.. as [i]hearsay[/i], hedged by distance and faction, exactly like the news CR records on its own. Your mod needs to know [i]nothing[/i] about CR's memory system, its prompts, or its save format.

Facts can also [b]change[/b]. For things that aren't one-off events but standing state.. a settlement that moves, a character renamed, a place that ceases to exist.. [i]Feed.Update(...)[/i] replaces a previous entry by its id, and [i]Feed.Retract(id)[/i] removes one that is no longer true, so the world's gossip stays current instead of repeating something stale.

It is deliberately [b]one-way and guarded[/b].. you push facts in through one door, and never read or mutate CR's internals. That door validates everything it is handed: it bounds the text, clamps the magnitude, drops heroes or factions it cannot resolve, de-duplicates by id, and rate-limits each source.. so a misbehaving feed can neither bloat a player's save nor flood the gossip. The data contract is a stable, plain-string DTO (no Bannerlord types in it), so it will not break under you as CR evolves.

The result is that CR can act as the [b][color=#e69138]narrative-memory bus[/color][/b] of an entire modlist: every mod that changes the world can give that change a memory and a voice, and the lords of Calradia will talk about it. Openness as a feature.. the opposite of a mod you can only use but never build on.

[b][color=#e69138]…and give the player a mission[/color][/b]
The second door lets your mod set the player a [b]mission[/b], and CR runs the whole ceremony around it:

[code]
CalradiaRemembers.MissionBoard.Register(new ExternalMission {
    Id          = "grandRaces_series_7",
    SourceMod   = "GrandRaces",
    GiverHeroId = "lord_caladog",          // the NPC who frames and rewards it
    Title       = "Win the autumn racing series",
    Description = "Take the series at Pen Cannoc and the purse is yours.",
    RewardGold  = 5000,
    RewardRelation = 5,
    DeadlineDays = 30
});[/code]

CR issues it as a real quest: the giver brings it up in conversation in their own voice, it mirrors into the vanilla journal, and a deadline/abandon/self-heal lifecycle keeps it tidy (no ghost quests, even from a third-party mod). The crucial line is [b]who decides it's done[/b]: YOUR mod owns the completion signal.. it calls [i]MissionBoard.Complete(id)[/i] when its own deed is truly finished (only it can know), and CR pays the gated reward when the player reports back to the giver. CR never pays on the AI's word, and never on yours alone.. the player still has to come collect. You define an arbitrary mission (a tournament series, a smuggling run, surviving a siege) without CR knowing your internals, and without weakening its "rewards only for real deeds" promise.

[b][color=#e69138]…and reach into what a single character remembers[/color][/b]
Beyond feeding the world, your mod can read and write ONE character's own memory. Ask CR what a given character already remembers (so you never inject a duplicate), and add memories it will carry into later conversations as its own recollection:

[code]
// what CR already holds for this character
IReadOnlyList<CrMemoryRecord> known = CalradiaRemembers.CrNpcMemory.GetMemories(npcId);

// give the character a memory it will treat as its own
CalradiaRemembers.CrNpcMemory.AddMemory(new CrMemoryEntry {
    NpcId     = npcId,                                   // a hero StringId, never a display name
    Summary   = "We agreed to meet at the ford at dawn.",
    Kind      = "Agreement",                             // optional; unknown/internal kinds become Other
    DaysAgo   = 0,                                       // 0 = today, dated back from now
    SourceMod = "MyMod"
});[/code]

A character is named by its hero StringId, never a fragile display name. An injected memory becomes an ordinary memory event, surfaced in later talks exactly like one CR recorded itself, and compacted over time by CR's own memory system. The same guard applies: the summary is bounded, and the handful of memory kinds CR relies on internally cannot be spoofed from outside.

[b][color=#e69138]…and listen to the conversation itself[/color][/b]
CR raises two events around every conversation, so your mod can act the instant one opens or closes:

[code]
// fires when a conversation OPENS, before the first line
CalradiaRemembers.CrConversation.Started += info => {
    // info.NpcId, info.SaveId (the campaign's stable id), info.GameDay
};

// fires when it CLOSES, with the whole attributed transcript
CalradiaRemembers.CrConversation.Ended += log => {
    foreach (CrTranscriptLine line in log.Lines)
        ; // line.SpeakerId, line.SpeakerName, line.IsPlayer, line.IsNarration, line.Text
};[/code]

[i]Started[/i] hands you the character's id, the loaded save's stable id, and the in-game day the moment a conversation opens.. early enough to recall and inject the right memories before the first prompt is built. [i]Ended[/i] hands you the full transcript, every line attributed to its speaker, including the third-party witnesses who spoke up.. so who-said-what is never a guess. Every subscriber runs under a guard: a handler that throws is isolated and can never break the conversation. (Read [i]CrNpcMemory.ApiVersion[/i] first if you reach these by reflection.)

If you would like to plug a mod into any of these doors, get in touch.. I would love to help.

[size=4]For modders: the NpcMemoryService SDK[/size]

Calradia Remembers is built on top of [b]NpcMemoryService[/b], an open SDK I designed from scratch to wire a large language model into a game so NPCs can hold real conversations and remember them across a whole campaign. The SDK is engine-agnostic (.NET Standard 2.0, no dependency on any game engine or UI toolkit), fully documented, and designed to be extended.

[b]As a player you don't need to install anything extra[/b].. the SDK is bundled inside this mod and works out of the box.

If you are a modder and want to build on this foundation.. add new conversation features, new action types, new memory behaviours, or wire the SDK into a completely different game.. the SDK is available separately here:

[url=https://www.nexusmods.com/mountandblade2bannerlord/mods/11658]NpcMemoryService SDK on Nexus[/url]

It includes full XML documentation on every public type, a runnable reference integration (ConsoleRunner) that shows the complete wiring end-to-end, and a clean architecture you can extend without touching the core. You publish the game actions you want the model to be able to request, implement the verbs yourself, and the SDK handles everything else: memory, prompting, parsing, compression, and persistence. Contributions and extensions are welcome.
