Version 1.34.7

Marry one of your own companions, and household who stay behind no longer feel forgotten. Your saves carry over untouched.

New
- Marry your own companion. You can now wed a wanderer you recruited into your clan, not only a born noble. Once your regard is high enough and neither of you is otherwise wed, the companion is raised to a lordship of your house and the marriage proceeds like any other. Blood relatives are always off-limits, and a marriage will not be arranged while either of you is bound up in an army. Should such a marriage later end, the former spouse (who had no birth house of their own) returns to the pool of wanderers you could recruit again, rather than lingering as a stranded noble.

Fixed
- A spouse or family who stays behind no longer feels left behind. Your wife, consort, or close kin who is keeping your household while you are away on campaign now understands that this is a married life split between road and hearth, not an absence they resent. Previously a household member apart could open a conversation as though you had abandoned them, counting the days since they last saw you; now they speak as one of your own house, awaiting your return. Household who ride with you were already handled; this covers those who stay home.
- Characters are no longer relentlessly cold, especially on lighter AI models. Players on smaller local models reported that almost everyone they met acted mean, with no good people anywhere. The guidance that shapes how every character behaves now states plainly that being guarded is not being cruel: a stranger who has given no offense is met with ordinary courtesy, and coldness or insult is a response to a real slight, a threat, or a bad history, never the opening manner toward someone who has done nothing. This restores the range of decent, warm, and neutral people the world is meant to have.
- Bandit tasks are findable, and reporting an unfindable one no longer earns a grudge. A "clear the hideout" task now puts its map marker on the actual lair instead of the nearby town; a "clear the bandits" task is only ever offered when bandits are truly roaming near that town; and if you tell the giver you could not find the target, they take it as a mild disappointment rather than a broken word, with no lasting grudge held against you.
