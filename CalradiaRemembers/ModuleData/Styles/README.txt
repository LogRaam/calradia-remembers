CALRADIA REMEMBERS, NARRATIVE STYLES

Every .txt file in this folder is a STYLE: a set of rules describing HOW the AI writes, not who the
characters are. The file name is the name you will see in the game's style picker, so "Novelistic.txt"
shows up as Novelistic.

A style is injected into every conversation, adult scenes included, and is shared by both models (the
roleplay one and the NSFW one). It never changes a character's personality, what they know, or what
they are willing to do: those are decided elsewhere and always win.

You can edit a style WHILE PLAYING: save the file and your next message uses the new rules, with no
restart. Switching style in the picker takes effect on your next message too.

IN GAME
  cr.style_ui on      shows the style button in the chat window's header
  cr.styles           lists every style found here, and which one is active
  cr.style <name>     picks one from the console
  cr.style_show       prints exactly what is being injected right now

WHAT SHIPS WITH THE MOD

WRITING YOUR OWN

Copy any file, rename it, edit the text. Plain English, in the second person ("Write...", "Keep...").
Aim for ten to twenty lines. A style is read on every message, so it should be dense, not exhaustive.

Two ways to write one, and they combine well:

1. DESCRIBE the qualities you want (sentence rhythm, how much is shown, what to avoid). Precise and
   predictable, but it takes more lines to pin a voice down.

2. ANCHOR on an author. Naming a well known writer carries syntax, rhythm and diction in three words,
   which is far denser than any list of rules. Three cautions:
     - Famous authors work; obscure ones produce a vague pastiche the model invents.
     - A name alone drifts into caricature, and the caricature becomes a tic over a long conversation.
     - An author's habits can fight this mod's format, so say what must survive.

THE HYBRID RECIPE (recommended)

  Write with the voice of <author>.
  <three to five concrete traits you actually want: rhythm, diction, what is shown, what is withheld>
  Keep the mod's conventions: spoken words in quotes, gestures in *asterisks*.
  <anything of that author's habits you do NOT want, named explicitly>
  Never let the manner become a tic: if you used a construction in your last reply, find another.

The last two lines are the important ones. The first protects the mod's format, the second is what
keeps a strong voice from turning into a mannerism by the tenth message.

A NOTE ON SHARING

A writing style is not something anyone owns, so anchor your files on whichever author you like, living
or dead. The names shipped here are simply famous enough that the model has a strong voice to follow.
If a style you enjoy sends you off to read the author it anchors on, so much the better. What you keep
on your own machine is your business.
